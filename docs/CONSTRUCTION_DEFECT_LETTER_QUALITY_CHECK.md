# Construction Defect Findings Letter - Quality Check Guide

**Purpose:** Quick reference to verify findings letters for construction defect cases meet attorney-quality standards

---

## Quick Identification: Is This a Construction Defect Case?

**Look for these keywords in the case:**
- Contractor, builder, construction company, general contractor
- Remodel, renovation, home improvement, rebuild
- Subcontractor, Notice to Owner, mechanic's lien
- Defective work, incomplete construction, substandard workmanship

**If YES → This is a Construction Defect case → Use checklist below**

---

## ✅ Quality Checklist for Construction Defect Letters

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #1: Implied Warranty Language
**Must Include:**
- ✅ Phrase: "implied warranty" or "implied warranty of workmanlike construction"
- ✅ Statement: "Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner"
- ✅ Application to case facts: Identifies specific defects (e.g., "defective framing", "incomplete work")

**❌ FAIL if:** Only mentions "Breach of Contract" without "implied warranty"

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #2: Florida Statute Chapter 558 Citation
**Must Include:**
- ✅ Cited by name: "Florida Statutes Chapter 558" or "Fla. Stat. Chapter 558"
- ✅ Appears in section header (if using numbered sections) OR in bullet point header (if using bullet format)

**Example Headers:**
- Numbered: "## 2. IMPLIED WARRANTY & CONSTRUCTION DEFECTS (Fla. Stat. Chapter 558)"
- Bullet: "• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558):**"

**❌ FAIL if:** No mention of "Chapter 558" anywhere in letter

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #3: 60-Day Notice Requirement
**Must Include:**
- ✅ Explicit statement: "60 days' written notice" or "60-day notice"
- ✅ Content requirement: "identifying the defects" or "describing the defects"
- ✅ Purpose: "giving them an opportunity to repair" or "opportunity to cure"

**Example:**
"You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair"

**❌ FAIL if:** Notice requirement not mentioned OR timeframe omitted

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #4: 45-Day Contractor Response Period
**Must Include:**
- ✅ Explicit statement: "45 days to respond" or "45-day response period"
- ✅ Content requirement: "proposed repair plan and timeline" or "repair proposal"

**Example:**
"The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline"

**❌ FAIL if:** Response period not mentioned OR timeframe incorrect

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #5: Prohibition on Filing Suit
**Must Include:**
- ✅ Explicit prohibition: "You CANNOT file suit" or "cannot pursue litigation"
- ✅ Condition: "without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process"
- ✅ Format: Should be emphasized (bold, caps, or both)

**Example:**
"You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process"

**❌ FAIL if:** No prohibition stated OR only says "should send notice" without explaining it's mandatory

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #6: Mechanic's Lien Analysis (IF APPLICABLE)
**When Required:** ONLY if case mentions "Notice to Owner" received from subcontractor/supplier

**Must Include:**
- ✅ Consequence chain format: Notice → Lien → Foreclosure → Outcome
- ✅ Ultimate consequence in ALL CAPS: "FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME"
- ✅ Statute citation: § 713.02 and/or § 713.06
- ✅ Timeline: "90 days from last work date to file lien" (if applicable)
- ✅ Owner liability warning: Florida law may hold owner liable even if paid general contractor

**Example Pattern:**
"You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber on October 15, 2024. This notice is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. In Florida, the subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. Once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. Foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved."

**❌ FAIL if:** Notice to Owner mentioned in facts but no lien analysis provided

---

### MANDATORY ELEMENT #7: Protective Action Checklist (IF APPLICABLE)
**When Required:** ONLY if recommendations include paying subcontractor directly to avoid lien

**Must Include:**
- ✅ Sub-header: "If you proceed with [action], we strongly advise:"
- ✅ Bullet list with at least 3 items:
  - Obtain written release of lien or lien waiver
  - Retain proof of payment (cancelled check, receipt, acknowledgment)
  - Use payment as part of damages claim for reimbursement from contractor

**Example:**
"If you proceed with payment to the subcontractor, we strongly advise:
• Obtaining a written release of lien or lien waiver from the subcontractor
• Retaining proof of payment (cancelled check, receipt, signed acknowledgment)
• Using this payment as part of your damages claim in pursuing reimbursement from the contractor"

**❌ FAIL if:** Recommends payment but provides no checklist for protection

---

## 🚨 Common Failures (What NOT to Do)

### ❌ FAIL Example #1: Generic Contract Analysis
**Problem:** Treats construction defect as simple breach of contract

**What it looks like:**
> "Here are the key points of our analysis:
> 
> • **Breach of Contract**: Under Florida law, a breach of contract requires proving the existence of a valid contract, a breach of its terms, and damages resulting from the breach. You have a strong case..."

**Why it fails:** No mention of implied warranty, Chapter 558, or pre-suit notice requirement

---

### ❌ FAIL Example #2: Missing Pre-Suit Notice
**Problem:** Recommends immediate demand letter without explaining Chapter 558 requirement

**What it looks like:**
> "We recommend sending a demand letter to LLW Construction demanding completion of the project or reimbursement."

**Why it fails:** Client may sue immediately, triggering dismissal for failure to comply with Chapter 558

---

### ❌ FAIL Example #3: Incomplete Lien Analysis
**Problem:** Mentions Notice to Owner but doesn't explain consequences

**What it looks like:**
> "Additionally, you received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber."

**Why it fails:** No consequence chain, no "FORCED SALE OF HOME" warning, client doesn't understand urgency

---

## ✅ PASS Example: Attorney-Quality Construction Defect Letter

**Key Legal Points Section (Excerpt):**

> Here are the key points of our analysis:
> 
> • **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558)**: Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. You may have claims under this implied warranty due to defective bathroom framing that required complete reconstruction, incomplete work despite receiving $100,000 in payments, and failure to meet industry standards.
> 
> **Pre-Litigation Requirements:**
> Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific steps:
> • You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair
> • The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline
> • You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process
> 
> This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.
> 
> • **Mechanic's Liens (Fla. Stat. § 713.02 and § 713.06)**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber indicating potential lien rights due to unpaid materials supplied for your project. This notice is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. In Florida, the subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. Once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien. Foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved.

**Recommendations Section (Excerpt):**

> Furthermore, you should reach out to Tibbetts Lumber and pay the amount owed under the Notice to Owner to avoid a lien being placed on your home. If you proceed with payment to the subcontractor, we strongly advise:
> • Obtaining a written release of lien or lien waiver from Tibbetts Lumber
> • Retaining proof of payment (cancelled check, receipt, signed acknowledgment)
> • Using this payment as part of your damages claim in pursuing reimbursement from the contractor

**Why it passes:** Includes all 7 mandatory elements
