<h2 style="font-size: 13pt; font-weight: bold; margin-top: 20pt; margin-bottom: 12pt;">Background</h2>

<p>On or about March 2025, Erik Devlin entered into the Contract with LLW Construction, Inc. for the construction and rebuild of his property. Pursuant to the terms of the Contract, Erik Devlin paid One Hundred Thousand U.S. Dollars and Zero Cents ($100,000.00) towards the project. However, as of March 2025, only Seventy Thousand U.S. Dollars and Zero Cents ($70,000.00) worth of work has been completed, leaving significant portions of the project unfinished. The Contract explicitly states that "Highlighted sections indicate unfinished work," which remains unaddressed by the Contractor.</p>

<p></p>

<p>It is undisputed that Erik Devlin has fulfilled his financial obligations under the Contract, having paid a substantial sum in reliance on the Contractor's promises. Despite repeated attempts to secure completion of the work, the Contractor has failed to perform, leaving the Client with an incomplete project and significant financial exposure. As you are aware, the contract price was agreed upon at One Hundred Twenty-Eight Thousand U.S. Dollars ($128,000.00), of which Erik Devlin has already paid more than three-quarters.</p>

<p></p>

<h2 style="font-size: 13pt; font-weight: bold; margin-top: 20pt; margin-bottom: 12pt;">Legal Analysis</h2>

<p>It is undisputed that the parties entered into the Contract on or about March 2025, and Erik Devlin has fulfilled his financial obligations under the Contract. Despite this, LLW Construction, Inc. has failed to complete the project, constituting a breach of contract. Under Florida Statute § 95.031, Erik Devlin has a five-year statute of limitations to bring an action for breach of this written contract. The breach is evidenced by the halt in work since March 2025, despite payment for more than half of the contract price.</p>

<p></p>

<p>The Contract specifically provides:</p>

<blockquote style="margin: 12pt 0 12pt 0.5in; padding: 12pt; border-left: 3px solid #cccccc; background-color: #f9f9f9; font-style: italic; line-height: 1.8;">
Article 5 titled 'Performance Obligations' states: "Contractor agrees to complete all work described in Exhibit A within the timeframe agreed upon. All work shall be performed in a workmanlike manner and in compliance with applicable building codes. Highlighted sections indicate work that remains incomplete."
</blockquote>

<p></p>

<p>Furthermore, under Florida Statute § 713.06, any construction lien rights that LLW Construction, Inc. might assert are limited to the contract price. Given that Erik Devlin has already paid more than the value of the work completed, any such lien would be improper. Additionally, any lienor not in privity with Erik Devlin must have served notice within 45 days of starting work per Florida Statute § 713.16, further negating potential lien claims.</p>

<p></p>

<p>In the event that LLW Construction, Inc. files for bankruptcy, Erik Devlin's claims for breach of contract would be subject to the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362. Erik Devlin would need to file a claim in bankruptcy court and potentially seek relief from the stay to pursue other remedies. However, given the clear breach of contract and the substantial sums already paid, Erik Devlin has strong grounds for recovery.</p>

<p></p>

<h2 style="font-size: 13pt; font-weight: bold; margin-top: 20pt; margin-bottom: 12pt;">Demand</h2>

<p>As such, let this correspondence serve as a formal demand that:</p>

<ol style="margin: 12pt 0; padding-left: 0.5in; line-height: 2.0;">

    <li style="margin: 6pt 0;"><strong>3.</strong> LLW Construction, Inc. addresses any bankruptcy-related concerns by confirming their financial standing and ensuring that Erik Devlin's interests are protected in any potential bankruptcy proceedings.</li>

## Print Preview

When printed, the letter will have:

- **Margins:** 1" top/bottom, 1.25" left/right
- **Font:** Times New Roman 12pt
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced throughout
- **Page Width:** 8.5" (standard letter)
- **Quality:** Professional, court-ready appearance

The print media queries ensure:
```css
@media print {
    body {
        padding: 0.5in 1in;  /* Adjusted for printing */
        line-height: 1.8;    /* Slightly tighter for print */
    }
    
    h1, h2, h3 {
        page-break-after: avoid;  /* Keep with content */
    }
    
    p, ul, ol {
        page-break-inside: avoid;  /* Don't break mid-paragraph */
    }
}
```
