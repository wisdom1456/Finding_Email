# Letter Structure: Before vs After Fix

## Construction Defect Case Example (4 Issues)

### ❌ BEFORE FIX - Wrong Format (Too Formal, Harder to Scan)

```
Dear Mr. Devlin,

I hope you are doing well. I wanted to follow up with a summary of our 
findings after reviewing the documents you submitted regarding your property 
and the ongoing dispute with LLW Construction, Inc.

As discussed, the primary concern is the incomplete construction work on 
your home, which has led to significant financial and legal complications.


1. FACTUAL SUMMARY

Based on our review, we understand that the issues began after you engaged 
LLW Construction, Inc. to rebuild your home for a total contract price of 
$128,000. You have paid $100,000 to date, but the contractor ceased work 
in March 2025, leaving the project incomplete with only $70,000 worth of 
work done.

Despite this substantial payment:

The contractor has not completed the project.

The work performed has been substandard, including defective framing in 
the bathroom.

Since March 2025—over 8 months ago—the contractor's communications have 
been sporadic.

Additionally, you have received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, 
a subcontractor, indicating a potential mechanic's lien due to unpaid 
amounts totaling $2,150.66.


2. IMPLIED WARRANTY & CONSTRUCTION DEFECTS (Fla. Stat. Chapter 558)

Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all construction work 
will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. You may have 
claims under this implied warranty due to defective construction and the 
contractor's inability to complete the project despite receiving substantial 
payments.

Pre-Litigation Requirements:

Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific 
steps:

You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the 
defects and giving them an opportunity to repair.

The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and 
timeline.

You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-
opportunity-to-repair process.

This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate 
litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.


3. BREACH OF CONTRACT

The contract with LLW Construction Inc. was for a total of $128,000, and 
you have already paid $100,000. However, the contractor has only completed 
$70,000 worth of work, indicating a breach of contract. This breach has 
resulted in financial damages to you, and you may seek remedies such as 
damages for breach or specific performance.


4. MECHANIC'S LIENS AND NOTICE TO OWNER (Fla. Stat. § 713.06)

You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber on [specific date]. 
This notice is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against 
your property. The subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to 
file a lien, meaning a lien could be recorded soon. Once a lien is 
recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce 
the lien and recover the amount owed. Foreclosure of a construction lien 
can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved.


5. BANKRUPTCY IMPLICATIONS

LLW Construction Inc. has filed for bankruptcy, which imposes an automatic 
stay on claims, affecting your ability to pursue breach of contract claims 
immediately. You must file a proof of claim in the bankruptcy proceedings 
and may seek relief from the automatic stay to pursue claims.


RECOMMENDED ACTION & NEXT STEPS

Based on the above, a negotiated resolution would likely be your most 
efficient and cost-effective path forward...
```

**Problems:**
- ❌ Too many numbered sections (5 total: sections 2-5 should be bullets)
- ❌ Visually cluttered with excessive section headers
- ❌ Hard to scan quickly
- ❌ Feels like a legal memo, not a client letter
- ❌ Doesn't match attorney-written examples

---

### ✅ AFTER FIX - Correct Format (Clean, Scannable, Attorney Style)

```
Dear Mr. Devlin,

I hope you are doing well. I wanted to follow up with a summary of our 
findings after reviewing the documents you submitted regarding your property 
and the ongoing dispute with LLW Construction, Inc.

As discussed, the primary concern is the incomplete construction work on 
your home, which has led to significant financial and legal complications.


1. FACTUAL SUMMARY

Based on our review, we understand that the issues began after you engaged 
LLW Construction, Inc. to rebuild your home for a total contract price of 
$128,000. You have paid $100,000 to date, but the contractor ceased work 
in March 2025, leaving the project incomplete with only $70,000 worth of 
work done.

Despite this substantial payment:
• The contractor has not completed the project
• The work performed has been substandard, including defective framing in 
  the bathroom
• Since March 2025—over 8 months ago—the contractor's communications have 
  been sporadic

Additionally, you have received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, 
a subcontractor, indicating a potential mechanic's lien due to unpaid 
amounts totaling $2,150.66.


Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558)**: 
Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all construction work 
will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. You may have 
claims under this implied warranty due to defective construction and the 
contractor's inability to complete the project despite receiving substantial 
payments.

**Pre-Litigation Requirements:**
Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific 
steps:
• You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying 
  the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair
• The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan 
  and timeline
• You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-
  opportunity-to-repair process

This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate 
litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.

• **Breach of Contract**: The contract with LLW Construction Inc. was for 
a total of $128,000, and you have already paid $100,000. However, the 
contractor has only completed $70,000 worth of work, indicating a breach 
of contract. This breach has resulted in financial damages to you, and you 
may seek remedies such as damages for breach or specific performance.

• **Mechanic's Liens (Fla. Stat. § 713.06)**: You received a Notice to 
Owner from Tibbetts Lumber on [specific date]. This notice is the FIRST 
STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. The 
subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien, meaning 
a lien could be recorded soon. Once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor 
may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the 
amount owed. Foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the 
FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved.

• **Bankruptcy Implications**: LLW Construction Inc. has filed for 
bankruptcy, which imposes an automatic stay on claims, affecting your 
ability to pursue breach of contract claims immediately. You must file a 
proof of claim in the bankruptcy proceedings and may seek relief from the 
automatic stay to pursue claims.


2. RECOMMENDED ACTION & NEXT STEPS

Based on the above, a negotiated resolution would likely be your most 
efficient and cost-effective path forward...
```

**Improvements:**
- ✅ Only 2 main numbered sections (1. FACTUAL SUMMARY, 2. RECOMMENDED ACTION)
- ✅ Legal analysis in clean bullet format with substantive paragraphs
- ✅ Much easier to scan and read
- ✅ Professional but approachable tone
- ✅ Matches attorney-written examples (Miguel Velasco, Balaji Badam style)
- ✅ Chapter 558 requirements integrated naturally, not as standalone section

## When to Use Each Format

### Simple Bullets Format (1-4 Issues) - DEFAULT

**Use when:**
- 1-4 primary legal issues
- Standard procedural requirements (Chapter 558, basic eviction notices)
- Most construction defect cases
- Most landlord-tenant cases
- Straightforward contract disputes

**Examples:**
- Erik Devlin (construction defect: 4 issues)
- Miguel Velasco (landlord-tenant: 3 issues)
- Balaji Badam (tenant debt: 2 issues)

**Structure:**
```
1. FACTUAL SUMMARY
Here are the key points of our analysis:
• Issue 1 (substantive paragraph)
• Issue 2 (substantive paragraph)
• Issue 3 (substantive paragraph)
• Issue 4 (substantive paragraph)
2. RECOMMENDED ACTION
```

---

### Numbered Sections Format (5+ Issues) - RARE

**Use when:**
- 5+ distinct legal theories
- Truly complex procedural requirements (multi-jurisdiction, specialized tribunals)
- Cases with many independent claims
- Complex regulatory matters

**Examples:**
- Christopher Eastman (7+ legal theories across jurisdictions)
- Complex bankruptcy adversary proceedings
- Multi-party construction disputes with cross-claims

**Structure:**
```
1. FACTUAL SUMMARY
Key Findings
2. FIRST LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
3. SECOND LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
4. THIRD LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
5. FOURTH LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
6. FIFTH LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
7. SIXTH LEGAL ISSUE (dedicated section)
8. RECOMMENDED ACTION
```

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Most cases (3-4 issues) = Simple bullets** (not numbered sections)
2. **Chapter 558 pre-suit notice = Standard** (not "complex procedure")
3. **Simple bullets = More scannable** for clients
4. **Numbered sections = Reserved for truly complex cases** (5+ issues)
5. **After fix: Better matches attorney-written examples**
