Key Legal Issues & Analysis

1) Breach of Contract and Related Theories

  • Breach of Contract: Incomplete or defective performance constitutes breach. Remedies include cost to repair/complete, refund of overpayments, and incidental damages reasonably flowing from breach.
  • Implied Warranties/Workmanlike Performance: Florida recognizes an implied duty to perform construction in a skillful, workmanlike manner; deviation supports breach.
  • Alternative/Equitable Claims: Unjust enrichment and quantum meruit may be pled in the alternative if the contract is challenged or deemed unenforceable in whole or part.
  • Misrepresentation/Conversion (to be investigated): If LLW misrepresented purchase or storage of materials (e.g., cabinets) or diverted funds, potential claims include fraudulent inducement, conversion, and, if facts support felonious intent, civil theft (Fla. Stat. § 772.11, which requires a pre-suit demand). These are fact-intensive and should be asserted judiciously.
  • Licensure Status: If LLW or Mr. Winiarek was unlicensed for the scope performed, LLW may be barred from enforcing the contract and may face penalties (Fla. Stat. §§ 489.127, 489.128).

2) Mechanic’s Lien Risk and Defenses (Fla. Stat. ch. 713)

  • Notices and Timing:
    • Subcontractors and suppliers not in privity with you must serve a Notice to Owner within 45 days of first furnishing labor/materials (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a)).
    • A Claim of Lien must be recorded within 90 days of the lienor’s last furnishing and served thereafter (Fla. Stat. § 713.08(5); § 713.18).
    • Liens expire in 1 year if not enforced by suit; you may shorten this to 60 days via a Notice of Contest of Lien (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(1)–(2)), or seek discharge via a show-cause procedure (Fla. Stat. § 713.21(4)).
  • Owner Payment Protections:
    • Owners should obtain progress and final lien waivers/releases from all lienors corresponding to each payment (Fla. Stat. § 713.20; § 713.06(3)(c)).
    • Before final payment to the contractor, you are entitled to a Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit listing all unpaid lienors (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(3)(d)); paying without this affidavit risks double payment.
    • For owners in privity with the contractor, lien exposure to properly noticed subs is generally capped at the unpaid balance remaining under the contract at the time of proper notice, provided payments were made appropriately and with releases (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(3)). Here, the maximum theoretical exposure to properly perfected lienors may approximate the remaining contract balance (~$28,335.77), subject to compliance and timing.
  • Owner Rights and Tools:
    • Demand for Sworn Statement of Account to lienors (Fla. Stat. § 713.16(5)) to verify sums due; failure to respond can impair the lien.
    • Transfer of Lien to Bond (Fla. Stat. § 713.24) to clear title if a lien is recorded (requires cash deposit or surety bond).
    • Notice of Contest of Lien (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(2)) to accelerate deadlines; Show-Cause procedure to seek court-ordered discharge if lienor does not timely file suit (Fla. Stat. § 713.21(4)).

3) FDUTPA – Unfair or Deceptive Practices

  • If LLW made material misrepresentations (e.g., about purchasing/storing cabinets, payment status of subcontractors, or quality standards) or engaged in deceptive/unfair practices, you may pursue claims under FDUTPA (Fla. Stat. §§ 501.201–501.213).
  • FDUTPA allows recovery of actual damages (generally out-of-pocket difference or cost to remedy) and discretionary attorneys’ fees to the prevailing party (Fla. Stat. § 501.2105). Punitive damages are not available under FDUTPA.

4) Damages and Proof

  • Direct Damages: Refund of payments for unperformed or defective work; diminution in value; cost to complete and correct to contract/specs.
  • consequential/incidental: Limited to those reasonably contemplated; document any temporary housing, water remediation, or protective measures if applicable.
  • Attorneys’ Fees/Costs: Potential fee recovery under the contract (if a fee clause exists), under the lien statute (Fla. Stat. § 713.29, prevailing party), and under FDUTPA (§ 501.2105).

B. Lien Risk Management

  • Track Deadlines: Calendar 45-day Notice-to-Owner windows and 90-day lien recording deadlines for each lienor (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a); § 713.08(5)).
  • If a Claim of Lien records:
    • Evaluate immediate recording of a Notice of Contest of Lien (60-day enforcement window) (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(2)).
    • Alternatively, pursue a Show-Cause discharge procedure (Fla. Stat. § 713.21(4)).
    • Consider transfer to bond (Fla. Stat. § 713.24) if lien impacts financing or closing schedules.
  • Payment Strategy: If you decide to pay any sums to resolve lien exposure, use joint checks and require statutory partial/final releases from each lienor for the precise through-date and amount (Fla. Stat. § 713.20). Do not release funds to LLW without contemporaneous lienor releases.
