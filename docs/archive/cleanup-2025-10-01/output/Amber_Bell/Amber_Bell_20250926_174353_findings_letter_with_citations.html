6. Legal Analysis

6.1 Breach of Contract / Failure to Perform

  • Law: Florida law imposes a duty on contractors to substantially perform in accordance with the contract and industry standards. The standard measure of damages for defective or incomplete construction is the reasonable cost of correction/completion, unless that would constitute economic waste. See Grossman Holdings Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037, 1040–41 (Fla. 1982) (adopting cost-of-repair measure).
  • Application: With approximately $100,000 paid and work allegedly materially incomplete/defective (notably the bathroom), you have a strong claim for breach. If a detailed punch list and independent expert report confirm defects and incompletion, a court is likely to award cost to complete/repair plus any consequential damages proven with reasonable certainty.
  • Defenses Anticipated: Contractor may assert substantial performance, owner interference, lack of notice/opportunity to cure, or that defects are de minimis. Well-documented notices, photos, expert opinions, and correspondence will be critical to rebut these defenses.

6.2 Defective Workmanship and Code Issues

  • Law: Contractors must perform work in a good and workmanlike manner and in compliance with the Florida Building Code. Where building code violations exist, injured parties may have a statutory cause of action under Fla. Stat. § 553.84.
  • Pre-suit Process: Many residential construction defect claims require compliance with Chapter 558, Florida Statutes (notice and opportunity to inspect/repair) prior to filing suit unless the contract validly opts out. See Fla. Stat. §§ 558.004–558.005.
  • Application: If the bathroom or other elements fail to meet code or industry standards, you may pursue breach and, where violations are established, relief under § 553.84. We recommend serving a Chapter 558 notice immediately (unless contracted out) to preserve claims and potentially facilitate settlement.

6.3 Unpaid Subcontractors and Mechanic’s Lien Exposure

  • Notices and Deadlines:
    • Subcontractors/suppliers must serve a Notice to Owner within 45 days of first furnishing labor/materials (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a)).
    • To perfect a lien, a claimant must record a Claim of Lien within 90 days of last furnishing (Fla. Stat. § 713.08) and sue to foreclose within 1 year of recording (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(1)). An owner may record a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten the foreclosure deadline to 60 days (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(2)).
    • Owners may demand a sworn statement of account from lienors (Fla. Stat. § 713.16) and should obtain appropriate lien waivers/releases as a condition of payment (Fla. Stat. § 713.20).
  • Contractor’s Final Affidavit: Before final payment to the contractor, the owner is entitled to a contractor’s final affidavit listing unpaid subs/suppliers (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(3)(d)). Do not make any final payment without this affidavit and corresponding lien releases.
  • Discharge/Transfer of Lien: Improper or expired liens can be discharged (Fla. Stat. § 713.21). Alternatively, an owner may transfer a lien to a surety bond to clear title (Fla. Stat. § 713.24) while disputes are resolved.
  • Attorney’s Fees: Prevailing party fees are available in lien actions. See Fla. Stat. § 713.29.
  • Application: Given the Notices to Owner you received, proactively demand sworn statements of account, collect conditional/unconditional lien releases corresponding to payments made, and record Notices of Contest for any recorded liens to compress their enforcement period. Consider lien transfer to bond if needed to protect the property during litigation.

6.4 Misrepresentation (Materials Procurement; e.g., Cabinets)

  • Law: Florida recognizes tort claims for fraudulent and negligent misrepresentation where there is an independent tort distinguishable from breach of contract. See HTP, Ltd. v. Lineas Aereas Costarricenses, S.A., 685 So. 2d 1238, 1239–40 (Fla. 1996). The economic loss rule does not bar independent torts outside products liability. See Tiara Condo. Ass’n, Inc. v. Marsh & McLennan Cos., 110 So. 3d 399, 407 (Fla. 2013).
  • Application: If the contractor represented that cabinets or other materials were purchased/stored (a material fact) to induce payment, and this was false, you may assert fraudulent or negligent misrepresentation. Such claims can support rescission of amounts paid for those items and recovery of consequential damages, and may also reach individual liability of Mr. Winiarek if he personally made the misrepresentations.

6.5 Damages and Remedies

  • Expectancy Damages: Cost to complete and cost to repair defective work, plus potentially consequential damages proved with reasonable certainty. See Grossman Holdings, 414 So. 2d at 1040–41.
  • Restitution/Disgorgement: Recovery of amounts paid for nonconforming or unfinished work; unjust enrichment may be pled in the alternative where appropriate.
  • Prejudgment Interest: Available from the date of loss for liquidated sums under Florida law.
  • Attorney’s Fees/Costs: By contract if there is a prevailing-party clause; by statute for lien litigation (Fla. Stat. § 713.29) and potentially under other statutory claims (e.g., § 553.84). We will review your contract for fee-shifting and notice/cure provisions.
  • Equitable Relief: Declaratory/injunctive relief to address lien clouds on title; orders to compel lien discharge if statutory defects exist.
