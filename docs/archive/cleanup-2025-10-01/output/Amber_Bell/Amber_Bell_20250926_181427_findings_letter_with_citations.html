V. Legal Analysis

A. Breach of Contract and Construction Defects

  • Elements: Valid contract, breach (incomplete/defective performance), and damages. The reported substandard bathroom work and undelivered cabinets support breach.
  • Measure of damages: Cost to correct and complete the work, plus reimbursement for payments made for work not performed or performed defectively. See generally Florida construction damages principles (cost to repair/complete unless economic waste).
  • Chapter 558 pre-suit process: For claims “arising out of construction defects,” you must serve a Notice of Construction Defects at least 60 days before filing suit (Fla. Stat. § 558.004). The contractor may inspect and must respond with an offer to repair, pay, or deny. We recommend serving a 558 Notice covering defective aspects (e.g., bathroom, any code violations), while simultaneously demanding contract compliance for incomplete work.
  • Building Code claims: If the work violates the Florida Building Code, a private right of action exists (Fla. Stat. § 553.84).
  • Licensure: We will confirm contractor licensure. Contracts by unlicensed contractors are unenforceable (Fla. Stat. § 489.128), which may affect leverage, remedies, and fee exposure.

B. Mechanic’s Liens and Owner Protections (Fla. Stat. ch. 713)

  • Notices and timing:
    • Subcontractors “not in privity” must timely serve a Notice to Owner (NTO) to preserve lien rights (Fla. Stat. § 713.06).
    • Claim of Lien must be recorded within 90 days of the lienor’s last work/furnishing (Fla. Stat. § 713.08) and generally must be enforced within 1 year unless shortened (Fla. Stat. § 713.22).
  • Owner defenses/tools:
    • Proper payments defense: Payments made in accordance with the statute and after obtaining required releases may protect the owner (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(1)).
    • Demand for sworn statement of account: You may demand a detailed accounting from a lienor; failure to respond within 30 days can impair their rights (Fla. Stat. § 713.16(2)).
    • Releases of lien: You may require lien waivers/releases as a condition to payment (Fla. Stat. § 713.20).
    • Final contractor’s affidavit: Required before final payment (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(3)(d)).
    • Shortening enforcement deadlines: Record a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten enforcement to 60 days (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(2)), or seek a show-cause order to require suit within ~20 days (Fla. Stat. § 713.21(4)).
    • Transfer of lien: You can transfer any lien to a bond to free the property (Fla. Stat. § 713.24).
  • Misapplication of funds: Knowingly failing to pay subs/materialmen after receiving draws can implicate Fla. Stat. § 713.345.
  • Attorney’s fees: Prevailing party fees are available in lien actions (Fla. Stat. § 713.29).

C. Unpaid Subcontractors; Indemnity/Allocation

  • If you paid the contractor but subs remain unpaid, we will pursue indemnity and contract remedies against the contractor and verify any NTOs, claim amounts, and dates.
  • We will seek voluntary resolutions with any lienors conditioned on proper releases to avoid double payment risk.

D. Consumer Protection (FDUTPA)

  • FDUTPA prohibits unfair or deceptive acts (Fla. Stat. §§ 501.201–501.213). Misrepresentations about materials (e.g., cabinets being ordered or stored when not) or billing for undelivered items may support a claim.
  • Remedies include actual damages and potential attorney’s fees (§ 501.2105). No pre-suit notice is required, though settlement demand is advisable.

E. Potential Civil Theft/Conversion (as warranted)

  • If funds were intentionally taken for cabinets/materials with no intent to deliver, a civil theft claim may be explored (Fla. Stat. § 772.11; predicate theft under § 812.014). This requires a pre-suit 30-day demand and clear and convincing evidence. We will assess after reviewing purchase documentation and communications.

F. Insurance and Regulatory Avenues

  • Contractor’s CGL coverage: We will request policy information and tender claims to the contractor’s carrier. Some resulting damage caused by subcontractors can be covered depending on policy language and Florida law.
  • DBPR complaint: If warranted, a complaint to the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) can create accountability and aid settlement leverage.
  • Florida Homeowners’ Construction Recovery Fund: For licensed contractors, the fund may offer recovery after you obtain a qualifying final judgment (Fla. Stat. §§ 489.140–489.144).

G. Statutes of Limitation and Repose

  • Breach of written contract: 5 years (Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b)).
  • Construction defects: Generally 4-year limitations period, with a 7-year statute of repose applicable to certain construction defect claims as amended in 2023 (Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(c)). Triggers and accrual can be fact-dependent; we will calendar conservatively.

VI. Damages and Remedies

  • Cost to correct/complete work via an independent licensed contractor (supported by expert estimates).
  • Incidental/consequential damages where recoverable under the contract and law.
  • Attorney’s fees and costs if provided in the contract or statutes (e.g., FDUTPA, ch. 713).
  • Equitable relief (e.g., injunctions, lien discharge orders) as necessary.
