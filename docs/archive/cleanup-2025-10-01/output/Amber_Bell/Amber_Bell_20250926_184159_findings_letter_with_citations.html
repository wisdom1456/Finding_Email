Legal Analysis

1) Breach of Contract (Incomplete/Substandard Performance)

  • LLW Construction’s failure to complete contracted work and delivery of substandard workmanship constitute breach. Florida law entitles you to recover the reasonable cost to repair defects and complete the project, less any unpaid contract balance, plus foreseeable consequential damages.
  • Evidence needed: the contract (and any change orders), payment applications, communications, photographs, videos, third-party expert inspection report estimating cost to correct/complete, and building permit records.

2) Mechanic’s Lien Exposure and Owner Protections (Ch. 713)

  • If a subcontractor/supplier has served a Notice to Owner under § 713.06(2)(a), you must protect against lien exposure by:
    • Requiring lien waivers/releases for each draw per § 713.20, and
    • Withholding payments to the contractor to cover claims of lienors who served notices, unless provided proper releases (the “proper payments” defense under § 713.06).
  • If a Claim of Lien is recorded (§ 713.08), you may:
    • File a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten enforcement from 1 year to 60 days (§ 713.22(2));
    • Move to transfer the lien to a bond or other security to clear title (§ 713.24);
    • Demand a sworn statement of account from lienors (§ 713.16), and treat failure to respond within 30 days as grounds affecting lien enforceability.
  • Before any final payment, owners should insist on the contractor’s final affidavit listing all unpaid lienors (§ 713.06(3)(d)); in light of current disputes, no further payment should be made.
  • If a lienor is unpaid after serving a Notice to Owner, the owner may pay the lienor directly and deduct from sums due to the contractor, preserving the proper-payments defense (§ 713.06(3)).

3) Subcontractor Nonpayment

  • Nonpayment to subcontractors after your progress payments suggests potential misapplication of funds by the contractor and heightens lien risk to your property. Obtain conditional/unconditional lien releases from all known lienors for amounts paid to date.
  • Use § 713.16 to demand contract copies and a sworn accounting from any lienor who served a Notice to Owner (and from the contractor as a lienor) to force transparency on sums owed/paid.

4) Potential FDUTPA and Misrepresentation

  • If LLW Construction or Mr. Winiarek represented that cabinets were purchased/stored and drew payment on that basis, yet you were forced to re-purchase, those facts support claims for deceptive or unfair practices under Fla. Stat. § 501.204, along with common-law misrepresentation.
  • FDUTPA remedies include actual damages and potential attorney’s fees to the prevailing party (Fla. Stat. § 501.2105). Document all representations, invoices, and proof of your out-of-pocket cabinet purchase.

5) Contractor Licensing/Regulatory Compliance

  • Verify LLW Construction’s license status and any qualifying agent, and confirm permit pulls. Contracts performed by an unlicensed contractor are unenforceable in law or equity by that contractor (Fla. Stat. § 489.128). Unlicensed activity can also constitute a FDUTPA violation.
  • If unlicensed work occurred, it may bolster restitutionary remedies and defenses to contractor claims. Retain permit records and DBPR license printouts.

6) Construction Defects Pre-Suit Process (Ch. 558)

  • For claims alleging construction defects, you must serve a Chapter 558 Notice before filing suit against the contractor and potentially implicated subcontractors (Fla. Stat. § 558.004). The notice describes claimed defects and allows inspection and an opportunity to repair or offer settlement.
  • We will draft and serve a compliant 558 Notice, coordinating with your expert to identify defects and repair scopes. Note: 558 does not bar parallel lien defense measures.

7) Unjust Enrichment/Alternative Theories

  • Pled in the alternative to contract claims, unjust enrichment may allow recovery where the contractor retained payments for unperformed or defective work. If any portion of the contract is unenforceable (e.g., unlicensed), restitution may be particularly viable.

8) Materials and Possessory Remedies (Cabinets)

  • If the contractor is withholding owner-paid materials or falsely claimed to have purchased/stored them, we will demand immediate turnover or proof of purchase and location. If necessary, we may pursue replevin (Chapter 78, Florida Statutes) or injunctive relief to secure the property.

9) Damages and Attorney’s Fees

  • Damages categories:
    • Cost to complete and repair defective work (supported by expert estimate and replacement contractor bids);
    • Reimbursement of amounts paid for unperformed or deficient work (~$100,000 paid to date, subject to offset for value-in-place);
    • Out-of-pocket expenses (e.g., cabinet costs, storage, temporary accommodations if applicable);
    • Consequential damages reasonably contemplated at contracting.
  • Attorney’s fees/costs: Available by contract (check fee-shifting clause), in lien actions (§ 713.29), and under FDUTPA (§ 501.2105). If unlicensed activity is established, additional statutory remedies and defenses apply under Chapter 489.
