Governing Law and Key Authorities

Contract and Remedies

  • Florida contract law: Damages are generally measured by the reasonable cost to bring the work into conformity with the contract, unless doing so would constitute economic waste. See Grossman Holdings Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037, 1039–41 (Fla. 1982).
  • Statute of limitations: Written contract actions: 5 years. Fla. Stat. §95.11(2)(b).
  • Construction defects: 4-year limitations period, with a statute of repose now generally 7 years for most post-2023 accruals (legacy matters may have different repose periods). Fla. Stat. §95.11(3)(c) (as amended 2023). Chapter 558 pre-suit procedures apply. Fla. Stat. §§558.001–558.005.
  • Code claims: Private cause of action for Florida Building Code violations. Fla. Stat. §553.84.

Construction Lien Law (Chapter 713)

  • Preservation notice: Subcontractors/suppliers must serve a Notice to Owner within 45 days of first furnishing to preserve lien rights. Fla. Stat. §713.06(2)(a).
  • Claim of Lien: Must be recorded within 90 days of final furnishing; a copy must be served on the owner within 15 days of recording. Fla. Stat. §§713.08(5), 713.08(4)(c).
  • Foreclosure deadline: One year from recording, unless shortened by a Notice of Contest (60 days) or transferred to bond. Fla. Stat. §713.22(1)–(2); §713.24.
  • Owner protections: Proper payments defense if owner secures and relies on contractor’s lien releases and final payment affidavit before final payment. Fla. Stat. §713.06(3)(d)1.–3.
  • Sworn statements: Owner may demand a sworn statement of account from any lienor. Fla. Stat. §713.16(5).
  • Fraudulent liens: Willfully exaggerated or sham liens are actionable. Fla. Stat. §713.31.
  • Misapplication of funds (criminal): Contractor’s intentional failure to pay subs from construction draws can constitute misapplication. Fla. Stat. §713.345.

Consumer Protection and Administrative Remedies

  • FDUTPA: Unfair or deceptive acts in trade/commerce are actionable; remedies include actual damages and potential attorney’s fees to the prevailing party. Fla. Stat. §§501.204, 501.211, 501.2105.
  • Licensing/discipline: Complaints to the Florida DBPR/Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB). Fla. Stat. ch. 489.
  • Recovery Fund: Florida Homeowners’ Construction Recovery Fund may provide limited restitution after obtaining a qualifying final judgment against a licensed contractor. Fla. Stat. §§489.140–489.144 (eligibility and monetary caps apply; verify current limits).

Analysis

1) Breach of Contract and Defective Work

  • Reported conditions—unfinished scope, bathroom requiring complete redo, and out-of-pocket purchases—support claims for breach of contract and construction defects. The principal measure of damages will be the reasonable cost to bring the project into conformity with the contract, plus incidental costs reasonably incurred. See Grossman Holdings, 414 So. 2d at 1040–41.
  • To preserve and maximize recovery, an independent, licensed contractor or construction expert should prepare a written defects list and a cost-to-complete/correct estimate, with photographs and code citations where applicable. Potential Florida Building Code violations may support a claim under §553.84.
  • Chapter 558 requires service of a pre-suit Notice of Construction Defects providing the contractor an opportunity to inspect and respond before filing suit. See Fla. Stat. §558.004 (generally, contractor has 30 days to inspect and 45 days to respond for single-family residential claims).

2) Lien Exposure and Subcontractor Nonpayment

  • Subcontractor “Notice to Owner” letters are not liens; they preserve the right to record a Claim of Lien later. If recorded, a Claim of Lien clouds title and may lead to foreclosure if not resolved. See Fla. Stat. §§713.06, 713.08, 713.22.
  • Because you have already paid $100,000, we must immediately evaluate whether “proper payments” procedures were followed—i.e., collection of progress and final releases from all lienors and a contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit before making any final payment. Fla. Stat. §713.06(3)(d)1.–3. If not, some payments to the contractor may not protect you from sub liens.
  • Upon any recorded lien:
    • We can serve a Request for Sworn Statement of Account (Fla. Stat. §713.16(5)).
    • We can record a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten the lienor’s foreclosure deadline to 60 days. Fla. Stat. §713.22(2).
    • If needed to clear title, we can transfer a lien to a bond. Fla. Stat. §713.24.
  • Nonpayment to subs may signal misapplication of funds. While §713.345 is a criminal statute, factual development (including bank draw records and pay applications) can support leverage in civil negotiations and administrative complaints.

3) Consumer Protection and Additional Remedies

  • If the contractor made material misrepresentations, engaged in deceptive change orders, billed for work not performed, or concealed nonpayment to subs, FDUTPA may provide additional remedies, including attorney’s fees to the prevailing party. Fla. Stat. §§501.204, 501.211, 501.2105.
  • Verify licensure and any prior discipline of LLW Construction/Mike Winiarek. If licensed, a DBPR complaint can trigger investigation and potential discipline; if unlicensed, additional claims and penalties may apply. Fla. Stat. ch. 489.
  • Following a qualifying final judgment that remains unsatisfied, the Recovery Fund may provide partial restitution (subject to caps and conditions). See Fla. Stat. §§489.140–489.144.

4) Damages and Attorney’s Fees

  • Damages: Cost to complete/repair, reimbursement of unearned/duplicative payments, incidental costs (e.g., temporary facilities, storage, reinstallation), and prejudgment interest on liquidated sums from the date of loss. See Grossman Holdings, 414 So. 2d at 1040–41.
  • Attorney’s fees: Available if the contract contains a prevailing-party clause; by statute in lien actions (Fla. Stat. §713.29) and FDUTPA (§501.2105); sanctions for unsupported claims/defenses under Fla. Stat. §57.105 may be available depending on conduct.

Key Risks and Deadlines

  • Chapter 558 timing: Must serve a compliant pre-suit notice and allow the contractor the statutory inspection/response period before filing suit for construction defects. Fla. Stat. §558.004.
  • Lien timeline: Lienors may record within 90 days of final furnishing; foreclosure generally within 1 year unless shortened. Fla. Stat. §713.22. Timely responses (e.g., Notice of Contest) are critical.
  • Limitations/Repose: Contracts—5 years (§95.11(2)(b)); construction defects—4 years with a 7-year repose for most post-2023 accruals (§95.11(3)(c)). Confirm accrual dates to avoid time-bar issues.

See §627.4137 (insurer disclosure) as applicable.
