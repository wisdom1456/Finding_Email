Governing Law

Jurisdiction/Venue: Florida law applies; venue in Hillsborough County, Florida Circuit Court (amount in controversy exceeds $50,000).

  • Florida Contract Law: Written contract claims; five-year limitations period. See Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b).
  • Construction Defects: Four-year limitations period (statute of limitations) for actions founded on the design, planning, or construction of an improvement to real property (with discovery rule nuances); ten-year statute of repose. See Fla. Stat. § 95.11(3)(c).
  • Chapter 558 Pre‑Suit Process: Notice and opportunity to cure construction defects; tolls applicable limitations. See Fla. Stat. § 558.004.
  • Construction Lien Law (Mechanic’s Liens): Chapter 713, Part I (Fla. Stat. §§ 713.001–713.37) including Notice of Commencement (§ 713.13), Notice to Owner (§ 713.06(2)), Claim of Lien (§ 713.08), time to enforce/discharge (§§ 713.21, 713.22), transfer of liens to bond (§ 713.24), contractor’s affidavit (§ 713.06(3)(d)), waivers/releases (§ 713.20), and sworn statements of account (§ 713.16).
  • FDUTPA: Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, Fla. Stat. §§ 501.201–501.213 (prohibits unfair or deceptive acts; allows “actual damages” and potential attorney’s fees).
  • Potential Civil Theft: Fla. Stat. § 772.11 (requires 30‑day pre‑suit demand; treble damages for willful violations), and criminal misapplication of construction funds, Fla. Stat. § 713.345 (criminal statute; can support civil theories).

Legal Analysis

1) Breach of Contract and Failure to Complete

  • Material Breach: Failing to substantially perform, abandoning work, or delivering non‑conforming work constitutes material breach under Florida law. Remedies include expectancy damages measured by the reasonable cost to complete or repair to bring the project into compliance with the contract, subject to the “economic waste” doctrine. See Grossman Holdings Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037 (Fla. 1982).
  • Measure of Damages: For residential work, courts commonly award the cost to repair/complete, less any unpaid contract balance, plus consequential damages proven with reasonable certainty and prejudgment interest from the date of loss.
  • Proof: A third‑party expert report and detailed punch list with cost‑to‑repair and cost‑to‑complete will be central to quantifying damages.

2) Defective Workmanship; Chapter 558 Notice

  • Workmanlike Performance: Florida law implies a duty to perform construction in a reasonably workmanlike manner consistent with applicable codes and industry standards.
  • Chapter 558: If asserting defect claims, serve a written notice identifying each defect and supporting detail, allow inspection, and consider any timely cure offer. See Fla. Stat. § 558.004. The process generally tolls the limitations period during compliance. You may pursue breach of contract concurrently or afterward if not resolved.
  • Permitting/Code: Noncompliant or unpermitted work strengthens breach/defect claims and may support FDUTPA if misrepresented.

3) Subcontractor Nonpayment; Lien Exposure; Owner Protections

  • Notices to Owner (NTO): Subcontractors/suppliers not in privity must serve an NTO within 45 days of first furnishing to preserve lien rights. See Fla. Stat. § 713.06(2)(a).
  • Claims of Lien: A lienor must record a claim of lien within 90 days of final furnishing and serve a copy within 15 days. Enforcement must begin within 1 year of recording. See §§ 713.08(5), 713.22(1).
  • Owner Tools to Manage Liens:
    • Demand contractor’s list of subs/suppliers within 10 days. See § 713.06(3)(c).
    • Before any final payment, require a Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit listing all unpaid lienors. See § 713.06(3)(d).
    • Serve a Request for Sworn Statement of Account on any lienor. See § 713.16(2), (5) (noncompliance has consequences, including fee limitations and potential defenses).
    • Obtain partial/final lien waivers/releases from all lienors as a condition of payment. See § 713.20.
    • If a lien is recorded: (a) record/serve a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten the enforcement period to 60 days. See § 713.22(2); or (b) file a show‑cause action to require enforcement within 20 days. See § 713.21(4); or (c) transfer the lien to bond to free the property. See § 713.24.
  • Withholding Payment: Where you have notice of unpaid lienors, Florida law permits withholding sums sufficient to pay them. See § 713.06(2)(e).
  • Attorney’s Fees Exposure: Lien actions are fee‑shifting. See § 713.29 (prevailing party). Proper use of statutory tools mitigates risk and may support fee recovery.

4) Misrepresentation; FDUTPA; Civil Theft (Limited)

  • FDUTPA: If LLW/Mr. Winiarek represented materials (e.g., cabinets) were purchased or stored when not, that may constitute a deceptive act under Fla. Stat. § 501.204. FDUTPA allows actual damages and potential attorney’s fees. See Fla. Stat. §§ 501.211, 501.2105; cf. Rollins, Inc. v. Heller, 454 So. 2d 580 (Fla. 3d DCA 1984).
  • Civil Theft: If funds were obtained with the specific intent to deprive you permanently (e.g., taking designated material funds without purchase), you may send a 30‑day civil theft demand under § 772.11. Treble damages are possible but the standard is high; use judiciously and only with evidence of intent. Note: § 713.345 criminalizes misapplication of construction funds (supports complaints to authorities/DBPR).
  • Personal Liability: Corporate officers may be personally liable for their own torts (fraud, civil theft), even if acting for the company.

5) Remedies and Damages

  • Contract Damages: Cost to repair/complete to contract specification, minus any unpaid balance; consequential damages (e.g., reasonable temporary housing if proximately caused and proven); and prejudgment interest.
  • Alternative/Equitable: Unjust enrichment/quantum meruit pled in the alternative if the contract is rescinded or found unenforceable.
  • Statutory/Fees: Potential attorney’s fees under contract (if clause exists), lien litigation (§ 713.29), and FDUTPA (§ 501.2105). Consider Florida Homeowners’ Construction Recovery Fund (if contractor is properly licensed and criteria met). See Fla. Stat. §§ 489.140–489.144.

6) Procedural Considerations; Limitations Periods

  • Limitations: Five years for written contract (§ 95.11(2)(b)); four years for construction defect (§ 95.11(3)(c)), subject to statutory triggers and tolling; ten‑year repose (§ 95.11(3)(c)). A timely Chapter 558 notice tolls certain periods (§ 558.004(1)(f)).
  • Venue: Hillsborough County Circuit Court.
  • Evidence: Maintain a complete chain of documentation; avoid spoliation by preserving the site condition until inspected.

Risk Assessment

  • Strengths: Significant payment relative to incomplete/defective performance; early defects; corroborating subcontractor nonpayment allegations; strong statutory owner protections under Chapter 713.
  • Risks: Potential lien filings by unpaid subs; contractor counterclaims for unpaid balance or change orders; fee‑shifting in lien litigation; fact disputes about scope/changes; necessity of expert proof on defects/costs.
  • Mitigation: Immediate use of Chapter 713 disclosures/affidavits, lien waivers, and show‑cause/contest tools; third‑party expert documentation; early 558 notice to frame defects and toll limitations.
