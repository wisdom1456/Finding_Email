V. Legal Analysis

A. Breach of Contract and Abandonment

  • Under Florida law, an owner may recover expectancy damages for breach of a construction contract, commonly measured by the reasonable cost to complete and/or correct the work, less any unpaid balance of the contract price. See, e.g., W.W. Gay Mech. Contractor, Inc. v. Wharfside Two, Ltd., 545 So. 2d 1348 (Fla. 1989).
  • Evidence of material defects, code violations, and failure to meet specifications supports breach. Project delay and cessation of work can evidence abandonment, entitling you to hire a replacement contractor and seek damages attributable to LLW.
  • Independent tort claims (e.g., fraud in the inducement) may proceed if they are truly independent of the contract obligations; however, the “independent tort doctrine” limits tort claims that duplicate contract breaches. See Tiara Condo. Ass’n, Inc. v. Marsh & McLennan Cos., Inc., 110 So. 3d 399 (Fla. 2013).

B. Construction Defect Claims and Pre-Suit Notice

  • Florida’s Construction Defect statute, Chapter 558, generally requires an owner to serve a Notice of Claim on the contractor before filing suit for construction defects, allowing inspection and an opportunity to cure. See Fla. Stat. § 558.004.
  • Serving a Chapter 558 notice does not waive your right to damages; it is a condition precedent designed to facilitate resolution. Emergency repairs to protect the property may proceed notwithstanding the notice process.

C. Mechanic’s Lien Exposure and Owner Protections (Chapter 713)

  • Notices to Owner (NTO): Subcontractors/suppliers without direct contract with the owner must generally serve an NTO within 45 days after first furnishing to preserve lien rights. See § 713.06(2)(a), Fla. Stat.
  • Claim of Lien: A lienor must record a Claim of Lien within 90 days after its final furnishing of labor/materials and serve it thereafter. See § 713.08, Fla. Stat.
  • Duration and Contest: A recorded lien expires after 1 year unless a foreclosure action is filed. The owner can shorten this to 60 days by recording a Notice of Contest of Lien. See § 713.22(2), Fla. Stat.
  • Transfer to Bond: You may transfer a recorded lien to a surety bond or cash deposit to clear title. See § 713.24, Fla. Stat. This is optional and used when sale/refinance deadlines demand immediate title clearance.
  • Sworn Statement of Account: You may demand a sworn statement of account from any lienor. Failure to respond within 30 days can restrict lienor remedies (including attorney’s fees). See § 713.16(2), Fla. Stat.
  • Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit: Before final payment, the contractor must provide a sworn affidavit listing unpaid lienors. See § 713.06(3)(d), Fla. Stat. If not provided, it is a statutory noncompliance relevant to defenses and damages.
  • Notice of Termination: Owners may record a Notice of Termination in limited circumstances to cut off lien rights, but statutory prerequisites are strict; improper use can increase risk. See § 713.132, Fla. Stat. We recommend caution.
  • Licensing Defense: If the contractor was unlicensed for the scope of work at any time of performance, contract enforcement and lien rights may be barred. See § 489.128, Fla. Stat. We will verify LLW’s license status during the project timeline.

D. Damages and Remedies

  • Cost to Repair/Complete: Recoverable reasonable costs to correct defects and complete the scope to contract specifications and code, less any unpaid contract balance. See Wharfside Two, 545 So. 2d at 1351.
  • Reimbursement of Overpayments: To the extent payments exceed the value of conforming work, you may recover overpayments and consequential damages reasonably foreseeable at contracting.
  • Unjust Enrichment/Quantum Meruit (pleaded in the alternative) if the contract is unenforceable or rescission is pursued.
  • FDUTPA (Fla. Stat. §§ 501.201–.213): If deceptive or unfair acts are established (misrepresentations, false draws, bait-and-switch, or material omissions), you may claim actual damages and seek prevailing-party attorney’s fees. See § 501.211 and § 501.2105, Fla. Stat. Corporate officers may be individually liable for their own deceptive acts. See KC Leisure, Inc. v. Haber, 972 So. 2d 1069 (Fla. 5th DCA 2008).
  • Misapplication of Construction Funds: If paid funds were diverted rather than applied to subs/suppliers, criminal and civil remedies may be implicated. See § 713.345, Fla. Stat. (potential restitution); also consider administrative complaints to DBPR.
  • Attorney’s Fees: Fee recovery depends on contract provisions and statutes (e.g., FDUTPA; lien statute for prevailing parties). We will evaluate the contract’s fee clause.

E. Limitations and Deadlines (Selected)

  • Written contract claims: 5 years. See § 95.11(2)(b), Fla. Stat.
  • Construction defect claims: Generally 4-year limitations and 7-year repose (post-2023 amendment), with accrual triggers including completion, abandonment, or CO. See § 95.11(3)(c), Fla. Stat.
  • FDUTPA: 4 years. See § 95.11(3)(f), Fla. Stat.
  • Lien deadlines: As summarized above (NTO 45 days; Claim of Lien 90 days from final furnishing; 1-year enforcement unless shortened to 60 days by Notice of Contest).

VI. Risk Assessment

  • Evidence Risk: Moderate, as conditions can change quickly during corrective work. Undertake a thorough documentation protocol (photos/video, destructive testing documentation, expert inspections) before remediation.
  • Counterclaims: Expect LLW to allege owner-caused delays or nonpayment. Maintain a complete payment ledger, communications, and schedule impacts to rebut.
