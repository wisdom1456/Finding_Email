Legal Analysis

1) Breach of Contract and Defective Workmanship

  • Breach: The reported failure to complete contracted scopes and the presence of material defects constitute a likely breach of express contract terms. Florida law entitles an owner to expectation damages — generally the reasonable cost to repair and complete the work to contract specifications, less any unpaid contract balance.
  • Workmanship Standards: Contractors must perform in a workmanlike manner consistent with applicable codes and industry standards. Defective installations requiring redo (e.g., bathroom) support damages for cost of correction and associated consequential costs directly caused by the breach.
  • Attorney’s Fees: Fee-shifting depends on the contract’s attorney’s fee provision. Please provide the executed contract and any addenda to confirm fee provisions, warranty terms, dispute resolution clauses (e.g., arbitration/mediation), and governing law/venue clauses.

2) Construction Defects — Chapter 558 Pre-Suit Procedure

  • Notice Requirement: Before filing suit for construction defects against a contractor or subcontractor, owners must generally serve a Chapter 558 pre-suit notice describing each defect with sufficient detail, and allow an opportunity to inspect and offer to repair or settle (see Fla. Stat. § 558.004).
  • Timelines: After receiving notice, the contractor typically has 45 days to respond with a repair/settlement proposal; an owner must wait 60 days before filing suit (longer for association claims).
  • Strategy: We recommend issuing a comprehensive 558 Notice accompanied by a detailed defect list and supporting photos/reports. This can be coupled with a demand for cure and a parallel settlement demand. Compliance preserves claims and often facilitates early resolution.

3) Mechanic’s Lien Exposure and Owner Protections — Chapter 713

  • Notice to Owner (NTO): Subcontractors and suppliers typically must serve an NTO within 45 days of first furnishing labor/materials to preserve lien rights (Fla. Stat. § 713.06). Receipt of an NTO obligates the owner to ensure “proper payments,” including obtaining conditional/unconditional lien waivers as a condition of further payments.
  • Claim of Lien: A lienor must record a Claim of Lien within 90 days after final furnishing and serve a copy within 15 days (Fla. Stat. §§ 713.08, 713.10). A recorded lien must be enforced by suit within 1 year of recording, subject to acceleration: 60 days if you record a Notice of Contest (Fla. Stat. § 713.22(2)) or approximately 20 days via a summons to show cause (Fla. Stat. § 713.21(4)).
  • Owner Document Demands: You may demand a sworn statement of account and a list of subcontractors/suppliers and amounts due (Fla. Stat. § 713.16). Failure to respond can impair a lienor’s fee recovery and defenses.
  • Contractor’s Final Affidavit: Before any final payment, you are entitled to the contractor’s affidavit that all lienors have been paid or listing those unpaid (Fla. Stat. § 713.06(3)(d)). Do not issue further payment without compliant affidavits and lien releases.
  • Transfer of Lien and Title Clearance: If a lien is recorded, you may transfer it to a bond to free title (Fla. Stat. § 713.24) while disputing its validity. Improper or fraudulent liens can be discharged and may expose the lienor to damages (Fla. Stat. § 713.31).
  • Direct/Joint Payments: Once on notice of unpaid subs who preserved lien rights, owners can utilize joint checks or direct payment strategies consistent with § 713.06 to avoid double payment risk while withholding disputed sums for defective work.

4) FDUTPA (Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act)

  • Basis: Misrepresentations about qualifications, progress, quality, permit status, or improper billing can constitute unfair or deceptive acts (Fla. Stat. § 501.204).
  • Remedies: Actual damages (often measured by out-of-pocket loss or diminution in value) and discretionary attorney’s fees to the prevailing party (Fla. Stat. § 501.2105). FDUTPA claims can be pleaded in the alternative to breach of contract.

5) Restitution/Unjust Enrichment and Alternative Theories

  • Where contract performance is substantially defective, equitable remedies allow recovery of amounts paid that unjustly enriched the contractor beyond the value of work in place, plus the reasonable cost to correct/complete. These claims may be pled in the alternative to contract claims.

6) Subcontractor Nonpayment; Licensing and Permit Issues

  • Nonpayment: The subcontractor’s claim suggests LLW Construction failed to pay subs despite receiving owner funds. This can support breach, FDUTPA, and potential misapplication of funds arguments (see Fla. Stat. § 713.345).
  • Deposits/Start Deadlines: If LLW received substantial deposits and failed to timely pull permits or commence work, Fla. Stat. § 489.126 may be implicated. While primarily criminal in nature, it can provide leverage and be cited in demand letters.
  • Licensure: We will verify LLW’s license status with DBPR. Unlicensed contracting exposes the contractor to significant penalties and limits their lien rights.

Remedies and Damages

  • Expectancy (Contract) Damages: Cost to repair and complete to contract specifications, less any unpaid balance.
  • Restitution: Disgorgement of payments for work not performed or valueless due to defects.
  • Consequential Damages: Directly resulting, foreseeable costs (e.g., temporary housing if required by repairs), subject to contract limitations.
  • Prejudgment Interest: On liquidated sums from date of loss under Florida law.
  • Attorney’s Fees/Costs: By contract, by statute in lien proceedings (§ 713.29), and potentially under FDUTPA (§ 501.2105).
  • Equitable Relief: Declaratory/injunctive relief to discharge invalid liens; orders compelling release of liens upon bond or payment.
Urgency: Because a Notice to Owner has been served by at least one subcontractor, a Claim of Lien could be recorded at any time within the lienor’s 90-day window after final furnishing. Immediate protective steps are advised to avoid title cloud and to position for discharge if a lien is recorded.
