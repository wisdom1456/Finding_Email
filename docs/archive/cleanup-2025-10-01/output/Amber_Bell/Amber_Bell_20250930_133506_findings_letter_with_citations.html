Applicable Law and Authorities

1) Contract and Defect Claims

  • Measure of Damages: In Florida, the usual measure for defective construction is the reasonable cost of repair/correction to achieve the contracted result, unless that would constitute economic waste. See Grossman Holdings Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037, 1040–41 (Fla. 1982).
  • Breach of Written Contract Limitations Period: 5 years. §95.11(2)(b), Fla. Stat.
  • Pre-suit Notice for Construction Defects: Chapter 558, Fla. Stat., generally requires a Notice of Claim describing defects and an opportunity to inspect and offer to cure before filing suit for construction defects, unless waived by contract.

2) Florida Construction Lien Law (Chapter 713)

  • Notice to Owner (NTO) by Subcontractors: Must be served generally within 45 days of first furnishing labor/materials. §713.06(2)(a), (b), Fla. Stat.
  • Claim of Lien: Must be recorded in the county where the property is located within 90 days of the lienor’s final furnishing. §713.08(5), Fla. Stat. Service must comply with §713.18.
  • Duration and Enforcement: Lien extinguishes 1 year after recording unless suit to enforce is filed; may be shortened to 60 days by owner’s Notice of Contest of Lien. §713.22(1)–(2), Fla. Stat.
  • Discharge/Transfer: Lien can be discharged via payment, release, order of court, or transferred to bond or other security. §§713.21, 713.24, Fla. Stat.
  • Proper Payments and Affidavits: The owner’s “proper payment” defense hinges on compliance with NTOs, obtaining a Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit before final payment, and securing appropriate lien waivers/releases. §713.06(3), Fla. Stat.
  • Notice of Commencement: Recording helps manage lien priority and notice. §713.13, Fla. Stat.
  • Waivers/Releases: Lien rights may be waived by signed releases to the extent of payment. §713.20, Fla. Stat.
  • Fee Shifting: In any action to enforce a lien, the prevailing party is entitled to reasonable attorneys’ fees. §713.29, Fla. Stat.

Analysis

A. Breach of Contract and Defective Workmanship

  • Liability: Based on your account, LLW Construction failed to complete work and performed portions defectively (bathroom). If proven, this constitutes material breach. The contractor’s failure to correct defects after notice further supports breach.
  • Damages:
    • Cost to Repair/Complete: Recoverable per Grossman Holdings. Obtain independent estimates for full remediation and completion.
    • Reimbursement of Sums Paid: To the extent payments exceeded the reasonable value of work properly performed, recovery is available via expectation damages or alternative unjust enrichment/rescissionary theories.
    • Consequential Damages: If foreseeable at contracting (e.g., costs of temporary housing if the bathroom is unusable, if contemplated), these may be recoverable; subject to contractual limitations.
    • Interest and Fees: Prejudgment interest on liquidated sums; attorneys’ fees if the contract includes a fee clause (reciprocal by statute) or in lien litigation.
  • Chapter 558: Before filing suit for construction defects, serve a compliant Notice of Claim with detailed defect descriptions and supporting documentation, unless your contract validly opts out. This process can facilitate resolution while preserving claims.

B. Subcontractor Nonpayment and Lien Exposure

  • NTO vs. Lien: A Notice to Owner is not a lien; it preserves a subcontractor’s right to later record a Claim of Lien. If a Claim of Lien is recorded, strict statutory deadlines and service rules apply.
  • Owner Defenses:
    • Timeliness/Service: Challenge liens not recorded within 90 days of final furnishing, or not served per §713.18.
    • Scope/Amount: Dispute inflated or fraudulent amounts; inaccurate liens risk fee exposure to the lienor and sanctions under Chapter 713.
    • Proper Payment Defense: If you complied with NTOs, secured releases/waivers with each payment, and obtained the contractor’s final payment affidavit prior to final payment, you may limit liability to the unpaid balance properly due. §713.06(3)(c)–(d).
  • Owner Tools:
    • Notice of Contest of Lien: Shortens the lienor’s time to file suit to 60 days. §713.22(2).
    • Transfer of Lien to Bond/Security: Clears title by transferring the lien to posted security in a statutorily calculated amount. §713.24.
    • Demand Contractor’s Final Payment Affidavit: Required before final payment; may expose nonpayment to subs and guide proper payments. §713.06(3)(d).
