V. Analysis

A. Breach of Contract and Defective Workmanship

  • Florida breach elements are: (1) a valid contract; (2) a material breach; and (3) damages. See Friedman v. N.Y. Life Ins. Co., 985 So. 2d 56, 58 (Fla. 4th DCA 2008).
  • Substantial noncompletion and defective work (e.g., a bathroom requiring full redo) support a finding of material breach.
  • Florida recognizes an implied duty to perform construction in a skilled, workmanlike manner; defective work violates that duty and contractual performance standards.
  • Measure of damages for defective construction is typically the reasonable cost to repair or complete, unless such cost constitutes economic waste. Grossman Holdings, Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037, 1040 (Fla. 1982).
  • Prejudgment interest on liquidated sums is recoverable as a matter of law. Argonaut Ins. Co. v. May Plumbing Co., 474 So. 2d 212, 215 (Fla. 1985).

B. Mechanic’s Lien Exposure and Owner Protections (Fla. Stat. ch. 713)

  • Notice to Owner (NTO): Lien rights for those not in privity require service of an NTO. § 713.06. Receipt of NTOs obligates you to ensure proper payments and lien waivers/releases to avoid double payment risk.
  • Claim of Lien: Must be recorded within 90 days of final furnishing and served within 15 days after recording. § 713.08.
  • Owner’s Information Rights: You may demand a Sworn Statement of Account from any lienor (including the contractor) within 30 days. Failure to timely respond impairs a lienor’s rights and fee recovery. § 713.16.
  • Contractor’s Final Affidavit: Required from the contractor before final payment. § 713.06(3)(d)1.
  • Lien Duration; Shortening Period: A recorded lien expires after 1 year unless suit is filed; owners may record a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten the enforcement period to 60 days. § 713.22.
  • Transfer to Bond: Any lien can be transferred to a surety bond to clear title. § 713.24.
  • Waivers/Releases: Obtain partial/final waivers contemporaneous with payments. § 713.20.
  • Misapplication of Construction Funds: Using owner payments for other purposes may violate § 713.345 and is a powerful leverage point.

C. Licensing and Consumer Protection

  • Licensing: If LLW or Mr. Winiarek lacked required licensure for the scope of work, their contract claims may be unenforceable. § 489.128. DBPR/Construction Industry Licensing Board has disciplinary authority. § 489.129.
  • FDUTPA: Deceptive or unfair acts (e.g., misrepresenting that cabinets were purchased/“in storage” when not) are actionable. Elements: a deceptive/unfair act, causation, and actual damages. Rollins, Inc. v. Butland, 951 So. 2d 860, 869 (Fla. 2d DCA 2006); § 501.204. Remedies include actual damages and attorney’s fees to the prevailing party. § 501.211, § 501.2105.
  • Homeowners’ Construction Recovery Fund: If LLW is licensed and you obtain a qualifying final judgment/award, you may seek recovery from the Fund. §§ 489.140–.144.

D. Chapter 558 Pre-Suit Process (Construction Defects)

  • Before filing suit for construction defects, a claimant generally must serve a written notice identifying each defect and giving the contractor an opportunity to inspect and offer to repair or settle. § 558.004.
  • Deadlines: Contractor typically has 45 days to respond with an offer after the notice; the notice must precede litigation by at least 60 days (unless properly contracted around).
  • We recommend serving a Chapter 558 notice to preserve claims and foster potential early resolution, unless the governing contract validly opts out.

E. Potential Claims and Remedies

  • Breach of Contract (noncompletion, defective work, failure to pay subs as required by contract and law).
  • FDUTPA (misrepresentations or unfair practices; e.g., statements about purchased/stored cabinets).
  • Unjust Enrichment/Restitution (alternative to contract; recovery for benefits conferred without adequate performance).
  • Negligent Construction (failure to meet professional standards of workmanship).
  • Construction Lien Law Remedies/Defenses (to discharge or bond off liens; to compel sworn statements and releases).
  • Licensing/Administrative Complaints (DBPR/CILB), plus potential referral regarding misapplication of funds.
  • Fraud/Misrepresentation or Civil Theft (Fla. Stat. § 772.11) if evidence shows intentional misappropriation with felonious intent; this requires a pre-suit demand and presents a higher proof threshold. We reserve judgment pending further evidence.

VI. Damages and Recovery Model

  • Restitution/Refund: Recovery of payments for incomplete or defective work (~$100,000) to the extent not matched by conforming performance.
  • Cost to Repair and Complete: Reasonable costs to bring all work into conformity with contract, plans, and code, including redoing the bathroom. See Grossman, 414 So. 2d at 1040.
  • Out-of-Pocket/Incidental Expenses: Cabinet costs paid due to contractor nonperformance; inspection and temporary housing if directly caused and contractually or statutorily recoverable.
  • Prejudgment Interest: On liquidated sums from the date damages were due. Argonaut, 474 So. 2d at 215.
  • Attorney’s Fees and Costs: If provided by contract or under FDUTPA (§ 501.2105). Note: FDUTPA fees are discretionary to the prevailing party.

VII. Immediate Recommended Actions (Priority — Next 7–30 Days)

Act promptly to protect lien defenses and preserve claims. Calendar all deadlines in writing.
  1. Cease Further Non-Conditional Payments: Withhold additional payments until lawful releases and satisfactory remediation are secured.
  2. Demand a Sworn Statement of Account from LLW and any known lienors under § 713.16 (30-day response). Request: amounts due, for what labor/materials, and amounts paid.
  3. Confirm Licensing Status: Verify LLW/Mike Winiarek’s licensure and qualifying agent for the scope with DBPR. Preserve screenshots/certifications.
  4. Serve a Chapter 558 Notice of Construction Defects (§ 558.004) identifying all observed defects and incomplete items; include photos and expert notes.
  5. Independent Inspection and Scope: Retain a licensed inspector or third-party contractor to produce:
    • a written deficiency report with code references;
    • a detailed cost-to-cure and cost-to-complete estimate; and
    • photo/video documentation.
  6. Lien Risk Management:
    • Collect all Notices to Owner and any Claims of Lien. Note recording/service dates.
    • If a Claim of Lien is recorded, consider recording a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten enforcement to 60 days (§ 713.22(2)), or transfer the lien to bond (§ 713.24) if needed to clear title.
    • Obtain partial/final lien waivers in statutory form (§ 713.20) from all payees before any further disbursements.
  7. Contract Compliance/Termination Preparation:
    • Locate termination-for-cause and cure provisions (e.g., AIA-type 7-day notice). Issue a written Notice of Default and Opportunity to Cure referencing specific breaches and Chapter 558 notice, reserving all rights.
    • If cure is not timely and adequate, proceed to Termination for Cause and secure the site, materials, and records.
  8. Cabinet Funds and Materials Accounting:
    • Demand documentation proving any purchase/storage of cabinets and other materials (POs, invoices, warehouse receipts, delivery tickets).
    • If misrepresented, incorporate into FDUTPA and fraud theories; preserve receipts for your out-of-pocket cabinet purchase.
  9. Regulatory/Administrative Complaints:
    • File a complaint with DBPR/Construction Industry Licensing Board (licensing, discipline) attaching evidence.
    • Consider a consumer complaint with the Florida Attorney General (FDUTPA) and local consumer protection office.
  10. Preservation of Evidence:
    • Send a litigation hold to LLW to preserve job files, communications, and accounting.
    • Do not remove or repair critical defects until adequately documented, unless necessary for safety; if repaired, preserve samples and detailed records.

VIII. Anticipated Procedure and Strategy

  • Pre-suit Track: Serve Chapter 558 notice; allow inspection/offer window; concurrently negotiate resolution conditioned on lien waivers, completion terms, or refund and release.
  • Alternative Dispute Resolution: Review the contract for any mediation/arbitration clauses. If arbitration is required (AAA/JAMS), we will adhere to clause procedures and preserve statutory claims.
  • Litigation: If no acceptable cure/refund, file suit in Hillsborough County (or demand arbitration) asserting:
    • Breach of contract and implied warranty of workmanship;
    • FDUTPA (deception/unfairness, including the cabinet representations);
    • Lien law claims/defenses including discharge and damages for improper liens;
    • Unjust enrichment and, if warranted by evidence, fraud/civil theft.
  • Lien Disposition: If liens are recorded, pursue discharge by court order or transfer to bond to protect title.
  • Collection and Security: Seek prejudgment interest, attorney’s fees (by contract and/or FDUTPA), and use the Recovery Fund if applicable post-judgment.

IX. Risks, Defenses, and Considerations

  • Contractual Defenses: LLW may assert delays, owner interference, or unresolved change orders. Maintain a complete record of communications and site access to rebut.
  • Arbitration Clauses and Fee-Shifting: These may affect forum, timetable, and fee recovery; a prevailing-party provision can enhance leverage.
  • Economic Waste/Diminution-in-Value: In rare cases, the defense may argue cost-to-repair is disproportionate; our expert scope will address necessity and reasonableness.
  • Lien Compliance: Ensure your compliance (e.g., Notice of Commencement, proper payments, collection of releases) to preserve statutory defenses.
  • Chapter 558 Timing: Failure to observe pre-suit requirements (if applicable) can delay litigation; we will calendar all statutory deadlines.
