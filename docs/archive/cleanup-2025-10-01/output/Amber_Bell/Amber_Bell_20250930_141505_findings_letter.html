Governing Law and Standards

  • Florida Contract Law: A party’s failure to substantially perform or to meet specifications and code constitutes a breach, entitling the non‑breaching party to expectation damages (e.g., reasonable cost of correction/completion) and, where applicable, consequential damages.
  • Construction Defect Pre‑Suit Process (Chapter 558, Fla. Stat.): Requires pre‑suit notice and opportunity to inspect/cure before filing certain construction defect actions against contractors. See §§ 558.001–558.005, Fla. Stat. Generally a 60‑day notice for single‑family residential projects with contractor inspection rights within 30 days (§ 558.004).
  • Mechanics’ Liens (Chapter 713, Fla. Stat.):
    • Notice to Owner by lienors not in privity (§ 713.06). Owner payments made after receipt of a Notice to Owner must be properly protected with lien waivers/releases, or the owner risks double payment (§ 713.06(2)(a), (3)).
    • Claim of Lien must be recorded generally within 90 days of the lienor’s last furnishing (§ 713.08; § 713.06(1)). Lien expires after 1 year unless enforced (§ 713.22(1)), which can be shortened to 60 days by a Notice of Contest (§ 713.22(2)).
    • Owner can discharge liens via payment, settlement, or by transferring to a bond (§§ 713.21, 713.24).
    • Before final payment to contractor, owner is entitled to a contractor’s affidavit that all lienors have been paid (§ 713.06(3)(d)).
  • Contractor Licensing and Consumer Protection:
    • Ch. 489, Fla. Stat. (Contractor Licensing; DBPR/CILB oversight). Discipline may include license suspension/revocation for misconduct (§§ 489.101–489.129).
    • Misapplication of construction funds can be criminal (§ 713.345, Fla. Stat.).
    • FDUTPA – Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (§§ 501.201–501.213, Fla. Stat.) – prohibits unfair or deceptive acts; prevailing party fee‑shifting at § 501.2105.
    • Construction Industry Recovery Fund (§§ 489.140–489.144) may provide partial restitution against licensed contractors after obtaining a qualifying final judgment and exhausting collection.
  • Applicable Codes/Standards: Florida Building Code and common‑law duty to perform in a good and workmanlike manner; local Hillsborough County permitting/inspection requirements.

Legal Analysis

1) Breach of Contract and Defective Work

  • The reported incomplete and substandard performance, including a bathroom requiring complete reconstruction, indicates a failure of substantial performance [1]. The proper measure of damages is the reasonable cost to repair and complete the work to contract specifications and code, less any unpaid contract balance.
  • If the contractor materially breached, you may have the right to terminate for cause after providing contractually required notice and an opportunity to cure (if any), and to engage a replacement contractor to mitigate further loss.
  • Documenting nonconforming work with dated photographs, expert inspections, and building department observations will be critical to establish breach and quantify damages.

2) Construction Defects – Chapter 558 Process

  • Before filing most defect claims, you should serve a Chapter 558 Notice of Construction Defects (§ 558.004). The Notice should detail each defect, location, and the remediation you seek. The contractor has inspection and response rights within statutory timeframes.
  • If the contractor fails to make a reasonable repair or settlement offer, litigation or arbitration (if the contract mandates) may proceed after the notice period.

3) Mechanics’ Lien Exposure and Owner Protections

  • You have received lien‑related notices from subcontractors asserting nonpayment [1]. Under § 713.06, payments made to the contractor after receipt of valid Notices to Owner should be protected with appropriate partial/final lien waivers/releases or by joint checks to avoid double liability.
  • If any Claim of Lien is recorded, you may:
    • Demand a sworn statement of account (§ 713.16);
    • Shorten the enforcement period to 60 days by recording a Notice of Contest (§ 713.22(2));
    • Transfer the lien to a surety bond or cash deposit to clear title (§ 713.24);
    • Seek discharge for facial defects or noncompliance (§ 713.21).
  • Request and retain a contractor’s affidavit prior to any further payment (§ 713.06(3)(d)). If the contractor cannot verify lienors have been paid, you should withhold payment and consider paying lower‑tier lienors directly as permitted by statute after appropriate notices.

4) Licensing and Consumer Protection

  • We will verify LLW Construction and Mr. Winiarek’s license status, scope, and any prior discipline with DBPR/CILB. If violations are substantiated (e.g., abandonment, misrepresentation, incompetency), an administrative complaint may be filed (§§ 489.127, 489.129).
  • If representations were deceptive or unfair under FDUTPA, you may recover actual damages and seek attorney’s fees (§ 501.2105). Evidence of false statements, bidding practices, or billing for unpaid subcontractor work can support such claims.
  • If the contractor is licensed and you later obtain a qualifying judgment that remains unsatisfied, you may pursue restitution from the Construction Industry Recovery Fund (§§ 489.140–489.144).

5) Restitution/Unjust Enrichment

If the contract is rescinded or otherwise unenforceable, you may seek restitution for payments retained without conferring corresponding value. Alternatively, in addition to contract damages, courts often award recovery for the cost to remove and replace defective work improperly performed.

6) Damages Overview

  • Direct damages: cost to correct defects and complete work to code; reimbursement of amounts paid for nonconforming or incomplete work (~$100,000 paid to date) [1].
  • Consequential damages: foreseeable costs due to breach (e.g., alternative housing if necessary, permit re‑fees, inspection fees), subject to proof and contract terms.
  • Attorney’s fees/costs: available if provided in the contract, under FDUTPA (§ 501.2105), or in lien litigation (§ 713.29).
  • Prejudgment interest on liquidated sums from the date of loss.
