Legal Issues and Analysis

1) Breach of Contract – Incomplete/Substandard Performance

  • Elements: Valid contract, material breach by contractor, damages to owner.
  • Application: Allegations of incomplete and substandard work support a material breach claim where performance falls below contract specifications, industry standards, or code [1].
  • Remedies: Expectation damages (cost to repair/complete), incidental damages, and potential rescission/restitution depending on contract terms; attorneys’ fees if provided by contract or applicable statute.

2) Construction Defects / Code Noncompliance

  • Defective workmanship that violates plans, specifications, manufacturer requirements, or the Florida Building Code may constitute a breach and a statutory “construction defect.”
  • Florida’s pre-suit process under Ch. 558 commonly requires a written notice of claim and an opportunity to cure before filing suit for construction defects (§ 558.004). We recommend issuing a compliant notice unless a clear exception applies.

3) Mechanic’s Liens and Unpaid Subcontractors

  • Subcontractors/suppliers who are unpaid may record a Claim of Lien (§ 713.08) if they timely served a Notice to Owner within 45 days of first furnishing (§ 713.06(2)(a)). You reported unpaid subcontractors and lien exposure [1].
  • Owners have powerful tools:
    • Contractor’s Affidavit before final payment (§ 713.06(3)(d)); withholding payment until affidavit received is authorized.
    • Sworn Statement of Account from any lienor (§ 713.16).
    • Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten lien foreclosure deadline to 60 days (§ 713.22(2)).
    • Transfer of Lien to Bond to clear title (§ 713.24).
    • Notice of Termination of Notice of Commencement (if applicable) under § 713.132, subject to statutory preconditions and notice to lienors.
    • Lien Waivers/Releases from contractor and subs (§ 713.20).
  • Owners must avoid double payment; amounts owed to lienors may be protected by proper withholdings and direct payment if statutory procedures are followed (§ 713.06(3)).

4) Licensing and Unlicensed Contracting

  • If LLW Construction or any person acting as a contractor was unlicensed for the scope of work, they are barred from enforcing the contract and may be subject to penalties (§§ 489.127, 489.128). Owners may seek recovery of compensation paid to unlicensed contractors under § 489.128(1).
  • We recommend an immediate license/disciplinary check with the Florida DBPR for LLW Construction and Mike Winiarek.

5) FDUTPA (Unfair and Deceptive Practices)

  • Misrepresentations about quality, qualifications, schedule, payment of subs, or code compliance may support FDUTPA claims (§§ 501.201–.213). Remedies include actual damages and discretionary attorneys’ fees (§ 501.2105). Punitive damages are not available under FDUTPA absent separate torts.

6) Restitution/Unjust Enrichment and Rescission

  • In the alternative to contract damages, Florida law allows restitution/unjust enrichment where the contractor retained payments without conferring commensurate benefit or delivered defective/incomplete work. You seek reimbursement of approximately $100,000 paid to date [1].

7) Contract Termination for Cause

  • Material breach (nonperformance, defective work, failure to pay subs) may justify termination for cause, subject to any contract notice and cure provisions and applicable statutes.
  • Upon termination, owners generally may procure completion by others and recover the reasonable cost of completion and correction, less any unpaid contract balance, plus allowable consequential damages.
