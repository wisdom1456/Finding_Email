V. Legal Issues and Analysis

A. Breach of Contract and Substandard Work

  • Elements: A valid contract, material breach, and damages are required to establish breach under Florida law.
  • Damages: Florida adopts the measure of damages for defective construction as the reasonable cost of repair and completion or diminution in value where appropriate. See Grossman Holdings Ltd. v. Hourihan, 414 So. 2d 1037 (Fla. 1982); see also cases applying cost-of-repair for construction defects.
  • Application: Paying ~$100,000 on a $128,335.77 contract with incomplete and defective performance supports a claim for breach and recovery of the reasonable cost to repair/complete, plus consequential damages that were foreseeable (e.g., permit re-inspection fees, temporary housing if needed), subject to proof [1].

B. Construction Defect Claim (Chapter 558)

  • Pre-suit Notice: Before filing an action for construction defects, you must serve a written notice under § 558.004 describing defects (e.g., workmanship deficiencies in the bathroom and other affected areas) and allow the contractor an opportunity to inspect/respond.
  • Timeline: Contractor typically has 45 days to provide a written response and proposed remedy; suit cannot be filed until the pre-suit period elapses (generally 60 days for single-family/owner claims) unless exceptions apply.
  • Strategy: Issuing a detailed Chapter 558 notice now preserves claims, narrows issues, and can facilitate early resolution without waiving rights in parallel lien matters.

C. Lien Exposure and Owner Protections (Chapter 713)

  • Notices to Owner (NTO): Lienors not in privity (subcontractors/suppliers) must timely serve an NTO (generally within 45 days of first furnishing) to preserve lien rights. § 713.06(2).
  • Claim of Lien: Lien must be recorded within 90 days after the lienor’s last furnishing and foreclosed within 1 year of recording unless shortened by a Notice of Contest of Lien (60 days). §§ 713.08, 713.22(1)–(2).
  • Owner’s Right to Withhold/Require Releases: When served with NTOs, the owner may withhold payments unless the contractor provides releases/waivers from lienors to the extent of payments. § 713.06(3)(c)–(d). A contractor’s final payment affidavit is required before final payment. § 713.06(3)(d).
  • Demand for Sworn Statement of Account: You may demand a sworn statement of account from any lienor (and from the contractor) identifying amounts owed/paid, which can clarify exposure. § 713.16.
  • Discharge/Contest/Transfer: Liens can be discharged by satisfaction or court order (§ 713.21), contested by recording a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten enforcement time (§ 713.22(2)), or transferred to a bond or other security (§ 713.24) to free the property from encumbrance.
  • Fraudulent Lien: Willfully exaggerated liens are subject to sanctions and attorney’s fees; prevailing party fees apply in lien actions. §§ 713.31, 713.29.

D. Licensing and Consumer Protection

  • Licensing Discipline: The Construction Industry Licensing Board (DBPR) may discipline licensees for misconduct, including failure to perform or pay subs. § 489.129.
  • Unlicensed Contracting: If the contractor is unlicensed for the scope of work, contracts are unenforceable by the contractor, and criminal/administrative penalties may apply. §§ 489.127, 489.128. (Further verification needed.)
  • Consumer Protection (FDUTPA): Unfair or deceptive acts are actionable with potential recovery of actual damages and attorney’s fees. §§ 501.204, 501.211(2), 501.2105.
  • Misapplication of Funds: Criminal liability may arise for misapplication of construction funds. § 713.345. Section 489.126 also addresses failure to timely perform after receiving substantial deposits (evaluate applicability given work commencement).

E. Termination for Cause

  • Contract Procedures: Termination typically requires written notice of default with a cure period per the contract. Adhering to contractual termination provisions is critical to avoid wrongful termination exposure.
  • Lien Coordination: Termination does not extinguish existing lien rights; carefully coordinate termination with lien protections (releases, bonding-off, or contesting liens) and documentation of defects/nonperformance.

VI. Remedies and Damages Overview

  • Monetary Damages:
    • Reimbursement of payments for incomplete/defective work (~$100,000), subject to offset for the value (if any) of conforming work proven by the contractor [1].
    • Cost to repair and complete to code/contract standard, including hiring a replacement contractor and necessary re-inspections.
    • Incidental/consequential damages reasonably foreseeable at contracting (e.g., storage, temporary housing if required), as allowed by Florida law.
    • Prejudgment interest on liquidated sums from the date of loss, as applicable.
  • Equitable Relief:
    • Specific performance is uncommon in remodel disputes; however, injunctive or declaratory relief may be used to address lien issues or declare rights under the contract and Chapter 713.
  • Attorney’s Fees/Costs:
    • By statute in lien litigation (§ 713.29) and FDUTPA (§ 501.2105), and by contract if a fee-shifting clause exists.

VII. Risks and Constraints

  • Mechanics’ Liens: Subcontractor liens can encumber title; failure to timely address may force payment disputes into lien litigation.
  • Economic Loss Rule: Negligence claims for purely economic loss without property damage may be limited; focus on contract and statutory claims.
  • Notice/Timing Compliance: Strict statutory deadlines under Chapters 558 and 713 must be observed.

Immediate (Days 1–7)

  • Preserve Evidence: Photograph/video all defective and incomplete work; retain copies of the contract, change orders, payment proofs, communications, permits/inspections, and any subcontractor notices [1].
  • Suspend Further Payments: Do not issue further payments to LLW Construction until statutory lien protections are satisfied (releases/waivers) and scope/quality issues are resolved. §§ 713.06(3)(c)–(d).
  • Serve Chapter 558 Notice: Send a detailed § 558.004 notice identifying all defects (including the bathroom and any code issues), reserving all rights under Chapters 713 and 558.
  • Demand Sworn Statements:
    • Serve a § 713.16 demand for sworn statement of account on the contractor and any noticed lienors to identify amounts due/paid and verify who claims nonpayment.
    • Request a list of all subs/suppliers engaged and the status of each account.
  • Lien Monitoring:
    • Search county records weekly for any Claim of Lien filings.
    • If a Claim of Lien is recorded, evaluate recording a Notice of Contest of Lien to shorten the enforcement window to 60 days. § 713.22(2).
  • Independent Expert: Retain a licensed inspector or construction expert to document defects, code noncompliance, and an estimated cost-to-repair/complete.

Short Term (Days 8–30)

  • Notice of Default/Cure: Issue a contractual notice of default to LLW Construction detailing breaches and defects, with a reasonable cure period and strict conditions (proof of permits, schedules, qualified crews, and lien releases).
  • Mediation Readiness: Propose early mediation following the contractor’s § 558.004 response, if any, to avoid further delays and cost escalation.
  • Regulatory Complaints:
    • File a complaint with DBPR/Construction Industry Licensing Board regarding alleged nonperformance and unpaid subs (attach evidence).
    • Consider a FDUTPA complaint/regulatory referral if representations or billing practices were unfair or deceptive.
  • Lien Strategy:
    • For any asserted liens, evaluate bonding off (§ 713.24) to clear title if necessary for financing/refinancing or sale.
    • Pursue statutory discharge if liens are defective or willfully exaggerated (§§ 713.21, 713.31).

Medium Term (Days 31–45)

  • Termination for Cause: If cure is not delivered and defects persist, proceed with termination per the contract. Ensure contemporaneous documentation and that statutory lien protections are in place.
  • Replacement Contractor: Solicit bids from licensed, insured contractors to establish the cost of completion/repair (forms part of damages proof).
  • Litigation Filing: Prepare to file suit in Hillsborough County Circuit Court (amount in controversy exceeds $50,000) asserting:
    • Breach of Contract;
    • Construction Defects (post-§ 558 compliance);
    • FDUTPA (if supported by facts);
    • Declaratory relief regarding lien rights/obligations as needed;
    • Attorney’s fees and costs under statute/contract.
