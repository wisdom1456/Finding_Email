Legal Analysis (Summary)

Under Florida law, the homeowners may pursue multiple remedies. A claim for breach of contract supports damages measured by the cost to complete or correct the work, or the difference in value if appropriate. Claims for defective construction can seek specific performance (completion to contract standards), repair costs, and consequential damages. Equitable remedies such as rescission and restitution/unjust enrichment may be available where the contractor has been paid and failed to perform. Mechanic's lien notice and potential filings by subcontractors must be addressed promptly, because lien rights in Florida can attach to the property and complicate recovery and sale. Administrative complaints to the Florida Construction Industry Licensing Board or consumer protection agencies are viable parallel remedies to seek contractor discipline and possible restitution.

Assessment of Strengths and Risks

  • Strengths: Clear, documented payment history and a written contract provide strong support for breach and restitution claims (contract price $128,335.77 and payments of $100,000) .
  • Risk: Subcontractor lien notices can create priority claims against the property and may require short-term funds or bonding to clear, and potential statutory notice/procedure defenses available to the contractor may affect exposure.
  • Practical consideration: If the work is left unfinished and multiple subcontractors assert nonpayment, the cost to complete and correct the project may exceed remaining contract balance and will be recoverable only if proven and awarded by a tribunal or settled.
