Findings Letter

Case Name: Maria Rodriguez

Date: September 14, 2025

Detailed Analysis and Findings

Maria Rodriguez finds herself in a challenging position as both a homeowner and a board member of the Sunset Village Homeowners Association (HOA). This dual role has placed her at the center of a potential conflict of interest, particularly as the HOA moves forward with foreclosure actions against homeowners for unpaid assessments. Maria's primary concern revolves around the legality and fairness of the HOA's foreclosure procedures, which are governed by Florida Statute Chapter 720 and the HOA's own Declaration. As a board member, Maria has fiduciary duties to the association, but as a homeowner, she is also subject to the same rules and potential penalties as her fellow residents. This duality has prompted her to abstain from voting on foreclosure procedures, highlighting her awareness of the conflict of interest. The urgency of the situation is underscored by the financial implications of the foreclosure actions, which could lead to significant hardship for Maria and other homeowners. The board has already approved foreclosure proceedings for three properties, with delinquent assessments totaling $24,800. Although Maria is current on her assessments, she is deeply concerned about the foreclosure process, particularly regarding timelines and notice requirements under Florida law. In response to these concerns, Maria is seeking independent legal counsel to evaluate both her position and the HOA's procedures. She is also contemplating resigning from the board to eliminate any potential conflicts of interest. Her priorities are clear: she wants to understand her legal standing regarding her dual role and to ensure that the HOA's foreclosure process is both legal and just. The board has recognized the need to address these issues, as evidenced by discussions in recent meetings about enhancing assessment enforcement and implementing a conflict of interest policy. Maria's proactive stance in raising these concerns demonstrates her commitment to transparency and fairness, both as a board member and as a homeowner. To navigate this complex situation, it is recommended that Maria and the HOA take several steps. First, a thorough review of the HOA's foreclosure procedures should be conducted to ensure compliance with Florida Statute Chapter 720 and the Declaration. Additionally, advising Maria on the implications of her dual role and considering the drafting of a conflict of interest policy for the HOA could provide clarity and prevent future conflicts. Finally, assessing the notice and fee calculation procedures for any potential defects is crucial to safeguarding the rights of all homeowners involved. Given the potential financial impact and the need for prompt resolution, addressing these issues with urgency is paramount. Maria's situation highlights the delicate balance between governance and personal interest within community associations, and her actions could set a precedent for how such conflicts are managed in the future. ]]>
