Applicable law for this matter includes the community's Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CCRs), the applicable community development order or local land-use regulation subject to county enforcement, and statutes and case law governing HOA fiduciary duties and governance (state HOA statutes and common law principles). The CCRs typically govern alterations to common areas and establish the voting thresholds (here, a 75% approval requirement is alleged) required for material changes to common property [1].

If the Board altered a common area in a manner that the CCRs define as a material change without the statutorily or contractually required homeowner approval, the Board likely exceeded its authority and acted in breach of the CCRs. Such conduct may also constitute a breach of the Board's fiduciary duties if the Board failed to act in good faith, with reasonable care, and in the best interests of the association and its members.
