Introduction

This findings letter summarizes the facts provided, identifies the principal legal issues arising from those facts, and provides prioritized recommendations for immediate and near-term action. The clients are plaintiffs in this matter , and the matter has been designated as urgent/critical .

Summary of Relevant Facts

  • The affected homeowners are Shirley Tafoya-Versen and Harry Versen .
  • The HOA Board removed required boardwalks on Salt Box Lane, which are part of the common area established by the community development order .
  • The Board replaced the removed boardwalks with pea gravel .
  • The replacement created unsafe walking conditions that force pedestrians into the street .
  • The removal appears to be a material alteration of HOA common areas that, according to the governing CCRs, requires a 75% community vote before such changes may be made .
  • The county issued a violation for the removal of the boardwalks .
  • Instead of restoring the original boardwalks in response to the county violation, the HOA Board is seeking to amend the community development order without homeowner approval .
  • A petition signed by 80% of Salt Box Lane homes demanding reinstatement of the boardwalks was submitted and ignored by the Board .

Legal Issues Identified

  • Whether the Board's removal of the boardwalks constitutes an unauthorized material alteration of common area in violation of the CCRs .
  • Whether the Board failed to obtain the required 75% homeowner approval prior to effecting a material alteration to common area .
  • Whether the Board's actions (removal, failure to restore, and seeking an amendment without approval) constitute a breach of fiduciary duty to the homeowners .
  • Whether the unsafe condition created by removal and replacement with pea gravel gives rise to a public-safety enforcement obligation on the county's part and exposure to liability for the HOA .
  • Whether the Board's ignoring of an 80% petition constitutes a procedural or substantive violation of the HOA governance requirements and homeowners' rights .

Clients' Priorities and Desired Outcomes

  • Restore and replace the original boardwalks on Salt Box Lane .
  • Prevent any amendment of the development order that would permit removal of required boardwalks without homeowner approval .
  • Enforce the CCRs and the 75% voting requirement for material alterations .
  • Eliminate the safety hazard that forces pedestrians into the street and protect neighborhood safety .
  • Protect property values and neighborhood aesthetics .
  • Obtain recognition and remedy for any breach of fiduciary duty by the Board, if established .

Preliminary Legal Analysis

Based on the facts presented, the Board's unilateral removal of boardwalks that are expressly required by the community development order raises substantial legal issues. If the CCRs and governing documents require a 75% community vote to materially alter common areas, then a unilateral removal without that vote is prima facie inconsistent with those documents. Further, the county's issuance of a violation strengthens the factual predicate for demanding restoration rather than an amendment to permit a noncompliant alteration. The Board's attempt to amend the development order without homeowner approval and its refusal to respond to a petition signed by 80% of affected homeowners may also give rise to fiduciary-duty and procedural violations under typical HOA governance principles.

Immediate Risks

  • Ongoing safety risk to pedestrians while boardwalks remain removed .
  • Escalation of county enforcement actions or civil liability if the safety hazard is not remedied .
  • Loss of homeowner rights if the Board succeeds in amending the development order without the required homeowner vote .

Recommended Next Steps (Priority Order)

  • Send a formal demand letter to the HOA Board and property management requiring immediate restoration of the original boardwalks and citing the county violation; demand that the Board halt any effort to amend the development order until the matter is resolved. (Prepare within 48 hours.)
  • Request and, if appropriate, obtain an expedited meeting of the homeowners to enforce the CCRs' 75% voting requirement and to gather documentary evidence of homeowner positions, including the 80% petition .
  • Notify the county enforcement authority in writing that homeowners request enforcement of the existing development order and provide documentation of the Board's replacement of boardwalks with pea gravel .
  • Preserve evidence: take dated photographs of affected areas, collect written communications from the Board, and gather signed copies of the 80% petition .
  • Assess fiduciary-duty claims: if the Board refuses to comply, prepare a complaint alleging breach of fiduciary duty and violation of the CCR voting provisions, seeking injunctive relief to restore the boardwalks and to enjoin any amendment process that would circumvent homeowner approval.
  • Consider temporary injunctive relief to compel restoration if the county does not obtain prompt action and if the safety risk remains acute.

Remedies Sought

Primary remedies supported by the facts and clients' priorities include:

  • Mandatory restoration or replacement of the original boardwalks to conform with the community development order and CCRs .
  • A declaration that any amendment to the development order that would permit removal of required boardwalks cannot be adopted without the CCR-mandated homeowner vote (75%) .
  • Injunctive relief prohibiting the Board from altering common areas or amending governing documents until proper homeowner process is followed .
  • Where appropriate, monetary or equitable remedies for breaches of fiduciary duty and costs of litigation and enforcement.

Conclusion

The facts provided indicate an urgent situation that implicates enforceable governing-document provisions, county enforcement authority, and possible fiduciary breaches by the HOA Board. The recommended course is to commence immediate written demands and enforcement steps, preserve and assemble evidence (including the 80% petition), engage the county enforcement authority, and, if necessary, file for injunctive relief to compel restoration of the boardwalks and protect homeowner rights under the CCRs .
