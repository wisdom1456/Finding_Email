II. Statement of Relevant Facts

  • The plaintiffs in this matter are Shirley Tafoya-Versen and Harry Versen .

  • The boardwalks were required by the community development order that governs the development .

  • A petition from Salt Box Lane homeowners representing 80% of the homes on the street demanding reinstatement of the boardwalks was submitted and has been ignored by the Board .
  • Your stated priorities include immediate reinstatement of the original boardwalks, preventing the Board from unilaterally amending the development order, ensuring pedestrian safety and accessibility, enforcing the CCRs and required voting thresholds for material alterations, and preserving property values and neighborhood aesthetics .

III. Legal Issues Identified

  • Whether the Board's removal of the boardwalks constitutes an unauthorized material alteration of common areas in violation of the CCRs.
  • Whether the Board exceeded its authority by failing to obtain the required 75% community vote (or other specified voting threshold) prior to making the alteration.
  • Whether the Board's actions constitute a breach of fiduciary duties owed to homeowners under applicable HOA law.
  • Whether the Board may lawfully attempt to amend the development order or governing documents without homeowner approval, and whether such an attempted amendment can cure or validate past unauthorized action.
  • Enforcement implications of the county development order violation already issued by the county enforcement authority.
  • Potential public-safety, accessibility, and property-value consequences, including possible ADA or accessibility-related issues arising from removal of safe pedestrian routes.

IV. Legal Analysis (Summary)

Based on the facts as reported, the Board's removal of required boardwalks and substitution of pea gravel presents multiple legal problems. Ordinarily, HOA governing documents (CCRs and comparable development orders) restrict material alterations to common areas and require specified membership approval thresholds (often a supermajority) before such alterations may be made. Where a county development order mandates particular improvements, unilateral removal that violates that order also exposes the association to municipal enforcement actions and may not be cured by internal unilateral action of the Board without compliance with both municipal requirements and the CCR amendment procedures.

Further, the issuance of a county violation creates an independent enforcement pathway that plaintiffs can leverage to compel restoration and to prevent further unauthorized modification. The Board's attempt to amend the development order (or otherwise re-characterize the removal) without homeowner approval, particularly in the face of an 80% petition for reinstatement, raises further concerns regarding procedural fairness and possible breaches of fiduciary duty.

V. Potential Causes of Action and Remedies

  • Declaratory relief: Seek a judicial declaration that the Board's removal of the boardwalks violated the CCRs and the development order and that any attempted amendment without the required homeowner approval is invalid.
  • Injunctive relief: Seek a preliminary and permanent injunction requiring the Board to restore the original boardwalks and to cease unilateral amendments or actions to alter the development order without homeowner approval.
  • Breach of fiduciary duty: Assert claims against Board members if the record shows they acted without authority, in bad faith, or contrary to the best interests of the community.
  • Mandamus / administrative enforcement support: Coordinate with county enforcement to secure administrative remedies or compel restoration consistent with the county development order.
  • Quiet title / covenant enforcement: If appropriate under local law, pursue covenant enforcement actions to enforce the CCRs and the development order requirements.
  • Emergency relief: If pedestrian safety is immediate and serious, seek expedited temporary restraining order or temporary injunction to require immediate safety measures and restoration planning.

VI. Recommended Immediate Steps

  1. Demand letter: Send an immediate, written demand to the HOA Board (with copy to the county enforcement officer) demanding restoration of the original boardwalks and cessation of any attempted unilateral amendment of the development order. Reference the county violation and the 80% homeowner petition in the demand .
  2. Preserve evidence: Document the current condition (photographs, measurements), collect copies of all Board minutes, communications, and any documents reflecting the Board's rationale and any amendment attempts.
  3. Engage the county: Provide the county enforcement authority with documentation of the Board's refusal to comply and the homeowner petition to strengthen administrative enforcement .
  4. Seek injunctive relief if the Board refuses to comply within a short, formal deadline: Prepare pleadings seeking temporary and preliminary injunctive relief to compel restoration pending resolution on the merits.
  5. Consider claims against individual Board members if the record shows deliberate disregard of CCRs, improper self‑dealing, or bad-faith conduct.

VII. Litigation Strategy and Practical Considerations

Given the county violation and the strong homeowner support on Salt Box Lane (80% petition in favor of reinstatement), an efficient strategy is to combine administrative pressure with parallel litigation seeking immediate injunctive relief and declaratory judgment. The administrative record with the county will be useful evidence in court and may speed remediation. We should also consider seeking attorney's fees and costs to deter future Board overreach if the governing documents or statute authorize fee-shifting for enforcement actions.

Immediate demand, evidence preservation, coordination with the county, and preparation for injunctive relief are warranted to secure prompt restoration and to protect homeowner rights .
