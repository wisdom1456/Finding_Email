Executive Summary

The HOA Board’s removal of the community boardwalks on Salt Box Lane and replacement with pea gravel appears to be an unauthorized material alteration of common areas under your community’s Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions (CCRs), which require a 75% community vote for material alterations. The Board proceeded without the required homeowner approval, ignored a petition signed by approximately 80% of affected homeowners, and now seeks to amend the county development order without homeowner approval—despite a county violation already issued for the removal.

Based on the facts provided and assuming Florida law applies, the record supports claims for injunctive and declaratory relief to: (1) enforce the CCRs, (2) prevent further unilateral action, (3) require restoration of a safe, accessible pedestrian route, and (4) remedy fiduciary and procedural breaches. Parallel remedies are available under Florida’s Homeowners’ Association Act and the Fair Housing Act for disability‑related accessibility and safety concerns. Immediate preservation of evidence and prompt pursuit of emergency injunctive relief are warranted given ongoing safety risks and the Board’s attempt to modify governing approvals.

Background and Factual Findings

  • The HOA Board removed community‑required boardwalks on Salt Box Lane and replaced them with pea gravel.
  • CCRs require a 75% community vote for material alterations of common areas; no such vote occurred.
  • The County issued a violation for unauthorized removal; the Board is seeking to amend the development order to retroactively authorize changes.
  • Approximately 80% of Salt Box homeowners signed a petition demanding reinstatement; the Board disregarded it.
  • Your photos and videos show pedestrians forced into the street, creating safety and accessibility hazards for residents, including those with mobility disabilities.

Legal Issues and Analysis

1) Unauthorized Material Alteration of Common Areas (CCR Violation)

  • The replacement of boardwalks with pea gravel is a material alteration affecting form, function, and safety of a common‑area pedestrian route. Your CCRs require a 75% member vote; none occurred.
  • Unilateral action by the Board likely breaches the CCRs. Members may seek injunctive and declaratory relief to enforce these restrictions. See Fla. Stat. § 720.305(1) (injunctive relief; attorney’s fees to prevailing party) and ch. 86 (declaratory judgments).
  • Business judgment deference does not shield actions that contravene governing documents or statutory duties.

2) Breach of Fiduciary Duties by the HOA Board

  • Directors must act in good faith, with ordinary care, and in the best interests of the association. Fla. Stat. § 617.0830.
  • Ignoring an 80% homeowner petition, proceeding in defiance of the CCR voting requirement, and creating foreseeable safety and accessibility hazards evidence a failure to meet fiduciary standards.
  • Exposure includes equitable relief and, in appropriate circumstances, damages and fee shifting.

3) Attempted Amendment of the Development Order Without Proper Authority

  • Development orders are county instruments. While the Board can apply to amend, it cannot use a county process to bypass CCR voting requirements owed to members.
  • Any county approval remains subject to challenge if inconsistent with the comprehensive plan or adopted conditions. See Fla. Stat. § 163.3215 (30‑day window from rendition of the order).
  • Given the existing county violation for removing the boardwalks, the record favors maintaining the status quo and restoring compliance.

4) Public Safety and Disability Accessibility

  • Replacing stable boardwalks with pea gravel has created a hazardous, non‑accessible route, forcing pedestrians—particularly those with mobility impairments—into the street.
  • Under the Fair Housing Act and Florida Fair Housing Act, the HOA must make reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, and practices when necessary for persons with disabilities to use and enjoy their dwelling and common areas (42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B); 24 C.F.R. § 100.204; Fla. Stat. § 760.23(9)).
  • A reasonable accommodation here includes reinstating a stable, accessible pedestrian surface or installing an equivalent accessible path. Failure to accommodate exposes the HOA to federal and state fair housing claims and potential injunctive relief, damages, and attorney’s fees.
  • ADA obligations may also be implicated if the route lies within public right‑of‑way or is open to the general public; otherwise, fair housing laws are the primary vehicle for relief against a private HOA.

5) County Development/Regulatory Noncompliance

  • The county’s violation supports the conclusion that removal was unauthorized. Under Fla. Stat. ch. 162, enforcement may include daily fines, compliance orders, and liens.
  • You may actively participate in the code enforcement process to seek an order requiring restoration of compliant, accessible infrastructure.

6) Procedural Irregularities and Record Access

  • Board actions materially affecting common areas typically must be noticed and discussed at properly noticed board meetings; member votes must comply with Fla. Stat. § 720.306 and the CCRs.
  • Members are entitled to inspect official records, including contracts, bids, board minutes, engineering reports, correspondence with the county, and legal opinions, within 10 business days of a written request. See Fla. Stat. § 720.303(4)–(5).
  • Director recall is available if warranted by continuing misconduct. See Fla. Stat. § 720.303(10).

Evidence Assessment

  • Photographs and videos documenting pedestrians forced into the street and instability of pea gravel (high probative value).
  • Homeowner petition with ~80% signatures (demonstrates lack of consent and community harm).
  • County violation and related notices/correspondence (independent authority corroborates noncompliance).
  • Governing documents (CCRs; bylaws; rules) confirming 75% vote requirement for material alterations.
  • Development order and approved plans (to show boardwalk specifications as approved conditions).
  • Board records (meeting notices, minutes, emails, contracts, invoices, consultant reports, legal advice to Board) accessible via § 720.303 requests.
  • Medical/functional impact records supporting disability‑related accommodation needs (to support FHA claims and urgency).

Risk and Defense Considerations

  • Cost/maintenance defense: Financial preferences cannot override explicit CCR voting requirements or safety obligations. Injunctive relief remains proper.
  • Safety rationale defense: The county’s violation and the produced evidence indicate the change increased, not reduced, risk; any safety justification should be tested against objective engineering standards and ADA/FHA principles.
  • Post hoc development‑order amendment: County approval cannot cure internal CCR violations vis‑à‑vis members; it only addresses external regulatory compliance.
  • Business judgment rule: Does not protect decisions that violate governing documents or statutory duties (e.g., §§ 720.305, 617.0830).

Recommendations and Next Steps (Urgency: Critical)

Immediate (next 5–10 days)

  • Send statutory records demand: Written request under Fla. Stat. § 720.303(4)–(5) for:
    • All board minutes, notices, and votes concerning the boardwalk removal/replacement and development‑order amendment.
    • Contracts, bids, and invoices relating to demolition and pea gravel installation.
    • All communications with county staff/code enforcement and consultants (engineers/planners).
    • All legal opinions relied upon by the Board.
  • Litigation hold/preservation notice: Demand the HOA and vendors preserve all ESI and hardcopy documents (emails, texts, photos, videos, CAD drawings, change orders).
  • Fair Housing reasonable accommodation request: Formally request restoration of a stable, accessible pedestrian route (e.g., the original boardwalks or ADA‑comparable surface) as a reasonable accommodation under 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B) and Fla. Stat. § 760.23(9). Include specific mobility impacts and propose prompt interim measures (temporary accessible matting or barricaded path).
  • Pre‑suit demand and notice of dispute: Demand immediate cessation of efforts to amend the development order and reinstatement of boardwalks. Provide notice under Fla. Stat. § 720.311(2)(a) for mediation while reserving rights to seek emergency injunctive relief per § 720.311(2)(c).
  • Engage county authorities: File a written statement of interest with code enforcement and planning staff urging enforcement requiring restoration; request notice of any hearings and staff reports.
  • Prepare emergency injunction: Draft and be ready to file a complaint for declaratory and injunctive relief seeking a temporary injunction to:
    • Enforce CCR voting requirements;
    • Enjoin any development‑order amendment that circumvents member approval;
    • Require restoration of a safe, accessible pedestrian route pending final adjudication.
    Cite Fla. Stat. §§ 720.305(1), 720.311(2)(c), and ch. 86.

Short Term (10–30 days)

  • Mediation under § 720.311: Attend with a focused proposal—restore boardwalks or approved equivalent; adopt an accessibility plan; Board to acknowledge CCR violation and cover restoration costs; fees to prevailing members if litigation proceeds.
  • County proceedings: Monitor code enforcement hearings; if the county approves any amendment, calendar the 30‑day window under § 163.3215 to challenge an inconsistent development order.
  • Board governance remedies: If noncompliance continues, initiate director recall under § 720.303(10); demand D&O carrier notice of claim.

Litigation Strategy (if necessary)

  • Parties: Plaintiffs (you and aligned owners) vs. HOA and, if appropriate, individual directors (for equitable relief) and vendor entities (as needed).
  • Claims:
    • Declaratory judgment (Fla. Stat. ch. 86) interpreting CCRs.
    • Injunctive relief for CCR enforcement (Fla. Stat. § 720.305(1)).
    • Breach of fiduciary duty (Fla. Stat. § 617.0830 standards).
    • Fair Housing Act/Florida Fair Housing Act reasonable accommodation and interference claims.
    • Companion county law claims as appropriate; support code enforcement.
  • Relief sought: Temporary and permanent injunctions; order compelling restoration of an accessible route; declaratory relief; attorney’s fees and costs; monitoring/reporting to ensure compliance.
  • Proof package: Your media evidence; petition; CCRs; county violation; expert affidavit (civil engineer/ADA accessibility specialist) on safety/accessibility and feasibility of restoration.
