# Presentation Requirements for NotebookLM

## Purpose of This Document

This document provides explicit instructions to NotebookLM on how to generate the attorney adoption presentation for Harvey AI (Legal Document Analysis Portal). Follow these requirements precisely to create an effective, credible presentation that drives adoption among practicing attorneys.

## Audience Description

**Who They Are:**
Practicing attorneys at Bernhardt Riley who handle Florida and New Mexico civil litigation matters. They value speed, accuracy, and verifiable outputs. They are skeptical of technology that claims to "replace" legal judgment but receptive to tools that remove busywork.

**What They Care About:**
- Time savings on document review and intake
- Accuracy and verifiability of AI outputs
- Control over final work product
- Risk mitigation through citation verification
- Professional quality outputs suitable for client delivery

**What They Fear:**
- AI errors or hallucinations that damage client trust
- Loss of control over legal judgment
- Complicated systems that require extensive training
- Technology that creates more work than it saves
- Professional liability from relying on unverified AI outputs

**What Motivates Them:**
- Removing low-value work (document reading, fact extraction)
- Accelerating client delivery timelines
- Improving consistency across cases
- Reducing intake bottlenecks
- Maintaining professional quality standards

## Tone Requirements

### Confident, Not Hype

**Do:**
- State capabilities clearly and directly
- Use concrete examples and specific features
- Position as operational production software
- Emphasize time savings with realistic estimates

**Do NOT:**
- Use marketing language or superlatives ("revolutionary," "game-changing")
- Oversell capabilities or promise unrealistic outcomes
- Use exclamation points excessively
- Anthropomorphize the AI ("thinks," "understands," "believes")

### Concise, Not Verbose

**Do:**
- Use short sentences and clear language
- Favor bullet points over paragraphs
- Limit each slide to one main idea
- Remove unnecessary qualifiers and hedging

**Do NOT:**
- Write dense paragraphs on slides
- Include tangential information
- Use jargon without explanation
- Add content not traceable to source documents

### Operational, Not Theoretical

**Do:**
- Focus on what attorneys do (workflow steps)
- Use imperative language ("Create a case," "Upload documents")
- Describe actual features, not future roadmap
- Ground statements in documented capabilities

**Do NOT:**
- Discuss AI theory or technical implementation
- Speculate about future features
- Include aspirational goals
- Reference capabilities not documented in source materials

## Slide Count Target

**Target:** 10-12 slides total

**Rationale:**
- Attorneys are busy; presentations must be concise
- Meeting time is limited (15-20 minutes)
- Focus on essentials: what it is, how to use it, why to trust it, what to do next

**Absolute Maximum:** 15 slides

If you exceed 12 slides, consolidate content. Remove nice-to-have slides before removing must-have slides.

## Content Rules

### What to Include

**Only include information that is:**
- Explicitly documented in the four source documents provided
- Accurate as of January 2026
- Operational (features live in production)
- Relevant to attorney adoption and usage
- Verifiable against README, help documentation, or presentation notes

### What to Exclude

**Do NOT include:**
- Backend implementation details (API architecture, database schema)
- Code snippets or technical specifications
- Features that are "planned" or "coming soon"
- Speculative capabilities or untested features
- Marketing language or hyperbolic claims
- Personal opinions or editorial commentary
- Information not traceable to source documents

### Verification Standard

Before including any statement in the presentation, ask:
- Is this explicitly stated in the source documents?
- Is this feature live in production right now?
- Would an attorney need to know this to use the system?
- Can I cite the specific source document for this claim?

If the answer to any question is "no," remove the statement.

## Formatting Guidelines

### Slide Design

**Visual Hierarchy:**
- Slide title: Large, bold, clear
- Main points: Bullet points, not paragraphs
- Supporting detail: Smaller text, indented or sub-bullets

**Slide Content Limits:**
- Maximum 5 bullet points per slide
- Maximum 2 levels of sub-bullets
- Maximum 20 words per bullet point

**White Space:**
- Leave significant white space on each slide
- Do not cram content to fit more information
- If content exceeds limits, split into two slides

### Language Style

**Use:**
- Active voice ("The system generates findings emails")
- Imperative mood for actions ("Upload documents," "Review results")
- Concrete nouns ("attorneys," "findings emails," "citations")
- Specific numbers ("51 Florida statutes," "2-5 minutes")

**Avoid:**
- Passive voice ("Findings letters are generated by the system")
- Vague qualifiers ("some," "many," "often")
- Abstract concepts without concrete examples
- Technical jargon without plain-English explanation

### Citation and Verification Language

**Emphasize:**
- "Every fact cited to source documents"
- "Attorney review required before client delivery"
- "Validated against verified legal corpus"
- "You remain responsible for final output"

**Avoid:**
- "AI-powered automation" (implies replacement, not acceleration)
- "Fully automated" (false and risky)
- "No attorney review needed" (absolutely false)
- "Perfect accuracy" (unrealistic claim)

## Key Messages to Emphasize

### Control and Responsibility
"You remain responsible for final output. This tool accelerates your review, it does not replace your judgment."

### Verification and Trust
"Every fact is cited to source documents. Every statute citation is validated against verified legal corpus. You can verify any statement."

### Time Savings
"Remove low-value work. Findings letter starts 80% done. Typical analysis: 2-5 minutes."

### Simplicity
"Five steps. No training manual required. Designed for attorneys, not engineers."

## What NOT to Do

### Do NOT Invent Features

If a capability is not explicitly documented, do not include it. Do not extrapolate, infer, or assume features exist.

**Example of What NOT to Do:**
- "Harvey AI can predict case outcomes" (not documented)
- "Future updates will include automatic client emails" (not live now)
- "The system learns from your edits" (not documented)

### Do NOT Use Hype Language

Avoid superlatives, excessive enthusiasm, or marketing-style language.

**Example of What NOT to Do:**
- "Revolutionary AI technology transforms legal practice"
- "Game-changing innovation for attorneys"
- "Unbelievable time savings"
- "You'll love this feature"

### Do NOT Overcomplicate

Keep explanations simple and direct. Attorneys do not need to understand how the AI works; they need to understand what it does and how to use it.

**Example of What NOT to Do:**
- "GPT-5.2 uses transformer architecture with attention mechanisms"
- "Multi-stage pipeline with parallelized document extraction"
- "Supabase provides PostgreSQL with row-level security"

**Example of What TO Do:**
- "AI analyzes your documents and generates a findings email"
- "System processes multiple documents simultaneously"
- "All case data is secure and access-controlled"

### Do NOT Skip Limitations

Be explicit about what the system does NOT do and what jurisdictions are NOT supported. Transparency builds trust.

**Example of What TO Include:**
- "Not supported: Federal claims, criminal law, immigration, bankruptcy"
- "Optimized for Florida and New Mexico civil litigation only"
- "Attorney review required before any client communication"

## Success Criteria

A successful presentation will:

1. **Drive Usage:** Attorneys log in and create test cases within the first week
2. **Build Trust:** Attorneys understand they remain in control and outputs are verifiable
3. **Set Expectations:** Attorneys know exactly what the system does and does not do
4. **Enable Action:** Attorneys know the next steps (login, quickstart guide, Q&A session)
5. **Reduce Friction:** Attorneys perceive the system as simple and beneficial, not complex or risky

## Final Reminder

**Stick to the source documents.** If something is not explicitly stated in the README, help documentation, speaker notes, or quickstart guide, do not include it in the presentation.

**Focus on adoption, not technology.** Attorneys care about workflow efficiency and professional quality, not AI architecture.

**Be honest about limitations.** Transparency about what the system does NOT do builds more trust than overselling capabilities.

**Keep it simple.** Five steps. Ten slides. No jargon. No hype. Clear, confident, operational.

Generate a presentation that drives attorney adoption by being clear, honest, and focused on practical workflow benefits.
