# Attorney Letter Reference Examples

This directory contains three actual attorney-written findings emails that serve as the **gold standard** for our AI-generated letter tool.

## Purpose

These examples demonstrate the **actual structure, tone, and content** that attorneys use when communicating findings to clients. They serve as reference materials for:

1. **Training and refinement** of the AI prompt template
2. **Quality comparison** - measuring how closely AI-generated letters match attorney style
3. **Pattern identification** - understanding what makes an effective client findings email

## The Three Examples

### 1. `devlin_attorney_letter.txt` - Construction Defect Case

**Case Type:** Construction contract dispute, defective work, mechanic's liens  
**Key Features:**
- Clear factual summary with bullet points
- Substantive legal analysis on implied warranty (Chapter 558) and breach of contract
- Detailed lien foreclosure risk explanation
- Specific action items (A/B/C format)
- Protective framing throughout

**Word Count:** ~1,100 words  
**Structure:** 5 numbered sections + recommended actions subsections

## Common Patterns Across All Three

### ✅ Opening
- Personal greeting: "I hope you are doing well" / "I hope this finds you well"
- Context statement: "Following our review..." / "As discussed..."
- Clear statement of purpose

### ✅ Factual Summary
- Chronological narrative OR bullet point format
- Specific dates, amounts, and party names
- Client-focused language (second person: "you")

### ✅ Legal Analysis
- **Substantive bullet points** - Each bullet is 2-4 sentences with complete analysis
- Plain English explanation before statute citations
- Real-world consequences explained
- Format: "• [Complete paragraph explaining issue and application]"

### ✅ Recommended Action
- Brief and directive (1-2 paragraphs, or A/B/C format)
- Specific actions to take
- Potential outcomes mentioned
- NO extensive subsections

### ✅ Closing
- Call to action: "Please let us know..."
- Simple and professional

## What These Letters DON'T Have

❌ **No "Legal Claims Analysis" section** with:
- "Legal Elements: To establish X, you must prove..."
- "Application Analysis:"
- "Available Remedies:"

❌ **No "Procedural Requirements" section** with:
- Statute of limitations calculations
- Multi-step litigation timeline

❌ **No "Third-Party Considerations" section** with:
- Insurance investigation recommendations
- Potential counterclaims discussion

❌ **No "Case Assessment" section** with:
- "STRENGTHS" bullet list
- "POTENTIAL CHALLENGES" bullet list

These are **legal memo sections**, not client communication.

---

## Key Differences: Client Letter vs. Legal Memo

| Element | Client Findings Email | Legal Memo |
|---------|----------------------|------------|
| Length | 600-1,200 words | 2,000-2,500 words |
| Sections | 3-4 sections | 8+ sections |
| Legal Analysis | Substantive bullets | Structured elements breakdown |
| Recommendations | Brief directive | Extensive subsections |
| Assessment | Integrated throughout | Separate STRENGTHS/CHALLENGES |
| Tone | Warm, protective | Analytical, comprehensive |

---

## Usage Guidelines

When refining the AI prompt template:

1. **Structure** - Match the 3-4 section format, not 8-section memo format
2. **Length** - Target 800-1,200 words, never exceed 1,500 words
3. **Bullets** - Each bullet should be substantive (complete paragraph), not just a heading
4. **Recommendations** - Keep brief and directive, avoid extensive subsections
5. **Tone** - Warm opening, protective framing, simple closing
