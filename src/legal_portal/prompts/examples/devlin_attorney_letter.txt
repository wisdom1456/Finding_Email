Erik Devlin Findings Letter - Attorney Written Example
========================================================

Subject: Legal Review and Recommended Next Steps – LLW Construction, Inc. Dispute

Good afternoon Mr. Devlin and Ms. Bell,

I hope this message finds you well. Following our review of your documents and our recent discussion, I am providing a summary of the situation involving LLW Construction, Inc., along with legal considerations and our recommendations for moving forward.

1. FACTUAL SUMMARY

Based on our review, we understand that the issues began after you engaged LLW Construction, Inc. to complete repairs to your home located at 3414 South Belcher Drive, Tampa, FL 33629, following damage from Hurricane Helene. The total contract amount was $128,355.77, of which $100,000 has already been paid.

Despite this substantial payment:
• The contractor has not completed the project.
• The work performed has been substandard, including serious construction defects such as improper bathroom framing requiring you to reconstruct it yourself.
• Since January, the contractor's only communications have been requests for more money, despite minimal progress.
• The contractor has stated they are out of funds and require additional payment to continue.

Additionally, you have received a Notice to Owner from a subcontractor for over $3,000, indicating they were not paid by the contractor for their work.

2. IMPLIED WARRANTY & CONSTRUCTION DEFECTS (Fla. Stat. Chapter 558)

Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. You may have claims under this implied warranty due to:
• Defective or incomplete construction
• Failure to meet industry standards
• Structural and functional deficiencies

Florida Statutes, Chapter 558, requires the following steps before pursuing litigation:
• You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair.
• The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline.
• You cannot file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process.

3. BREACH OF CONTRACT CLAIM

You may also have a breach of contract claim. To prevail, you must show:
1. A valid contract exists (which it does),
2. A material breach occurred (e.g., failure to complete or properly perform the work),
3. You have sustained damages as a result.

The contractor's failure to perform as agreed, including leaving work unfinished and failing to meet specifications, supports a potential cause of action for breach of contract. You may be entitled to:
• Monetary damages
• Cost of hiring a replacement contractor
• Reimbursement for subcontractor payments
• Other remedies under the contract and Florida law

4. LIENS AND NOTICE TO OWNER (Fla. Stat. § 713.02 and § 713.06)

Subcontractors who have not been paid have the right to file a mechanic's lien against your property:
• The Notice to Owner you received is a statutory prerequisite to filing such a lien.
• If not resolved, the subcontractor may proceed to record a lien, which can cloud title to your property and create additional legal exposure.
• Even if you have already paid the general contractor, Florida law may still hold you liable for unpaid amounts to subcontractors.

You may need to pay the subcontractor directly to avoid a lien and then seek reimbursement from LLW Construction.

5. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS

We recommend the following course of action:

A. Issue a Letter of Representation and Demand Letter:

Our firm can send a formal letter of representation and demand letter to LLW Construction on your behalf. This letter will:
• Demand that the contractor complete the project as agreed;
• Provide formal notice under Chapter 558;
• Seek reimbursement for payments you've made or will make to subcontractors;
• Initiate a resolution process that may avoid litigation and protect your property rights.

B. Pay the Subcontractor to Avoid a Lien (and Potential Foreclosure Risk):

You have received a Notice to Owner from a subcontractor who has not been paid by LLW Construction. This notice is the first step toward filing a construction lien against your property. In Florida, once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. While this is not always immediate, the risk should not be taken lightly—foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the forced sale of your home if not resolved.

To protect your property interest and avoid this outcome, we recommend that you consider paying the subcontractor directly the amount owed—approximately $3,000+—to satisfy the underlying debt. This action would:
• Prevent the recording of a lien or facilitate its removal if already recorded;
• Avoid legal fees, interest, and costs that would accrue if the subcontractor proceeds with litigation;
• Protect your property title from encumbrances, which could impact your ability to refinance or sell;
• Allow you to maintain control over the dispute and avoid being caught in the middle of a payment conflict between the contractor and subcontractor.

If you proceed with payment, we strongly advise:
• Obtaining a written release of lien or lien waiver from the subcontractor;
• Retaining proof of payment (cancelled check, receipt, signed acknowledgment);
• Using this payment as part of your damages claim in pursuing reimbursement from LLW Construction, who failed in their contractual obligation to pay their vendors.

This proactive step helps mitigate your risk and strengthens your position in any forthcoming demand or legal action against the contractor.
