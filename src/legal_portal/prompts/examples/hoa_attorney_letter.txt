HOA Parking Violation - Attorney Written Example
=================================================

Subject: Follow-up Summary - Central Park Neighborhood Association Fine

I hope you're doing well. My name is Alex Cherna, Esq., and I am the attorney who will be assisting with your matter. I'm writing to follow up with a comprehensive summary of our findings after a detailed review of the documents you provided, which include the February 10 and July 9, 2025, violation notices issued by the Central Park Neighborhood Association, Inc., the subsequent July 24, 2025 fine notice, your extensive e-mail correspondence with the Association's property manager, and the Association's governing documents—including its Rules and Regulations, Member Rights and Responsibilities, and Violation, Hearing and Fining Policy.

It is our understanding that your central concern lies with the $1,000.00 fine imposed against your property following alleged parking violations—first involving your own vehicle in February, and later, your daughter's vehicle in July. You maintain that the fine not only lacks a valid factual basis, but was imposed through a process that fell short of the procedural standards established under both the Association's policies and broader principles of fundamental fairness. Specifically, you assert that your repeated and timely efforts to engage with the property manager, clarify expectations, and remediate any alleged infractions were consistently disregarded. As a result, you were effectively denied a meaningful opportunity to understand the nature of the violations, to participate in the appeal process, and to prevent escalation prior to the imposition of the maximum allowable fine.

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• The HOA's governing documents require a structured notice and hearing process prior to the imposition of any fine: Under the Central Park Neighborhood Association's Violation, Hearing and Fining Policy, a homeowner must receive written notice of the alleged violation and be given at least 14 days' advance notice of a hearing before a separate Hearings Committee, which has the sole authority to approve or reject the Board's proposed fine. The Committee may not modify or increase the fine. While homeowners are often given a discretionary opportunity to cure the violation—as reflected in your February 10 letter—this is not a formal, guaranteed 30-day window under the governing documents. Furthermore, although curing a violation before the hearing may be considered in determining whether a fine should be imposed, the policy does not strictly prohibit the HOA from imposing a fine if the violation was cured after the hearing notice was issued but before the fine became due.

• Your written communications demonstrate timely, good-faith efforts to clarify and cure the alleged violations: On February 10, 2025—the same day you received the first notice—you emailed the property manager requesting clarification as to whether angled parking was allowed so long as the sidewalk remained unobstructed. You also explained the practical reasons for that parking configuration and clearly stated that you were mindful not to block pedestrian pathways. No response was received. Similarly, on July 9, 2025—the day the second notice was issued—you emailed the property manager twice to explain that the vehicle in question (your daughter's) had already been moved. In those emails, you asked for the date of the photograph, clarification on the hearing process, and confirmation of any remaining action required. You followed up again on July 14 and July 17, reiterating those inquiries and asking whether attendance at the upcoming hearing was necessary. No replies were ever sent to these messages.

• The July 24, 2025 fine notice appears to have been issued without verification that the violation remained ongoing at the time of the hearing: According to the Violation Policy, the Hearings Committee is authorized to reject a proposed fine if the violation has been cured before the hearing or prior to the date the fine becomes due. This discretionary safeguard appears intended to incentivize prompt correction and avoid excessive penalties. In your case, you confirmed—weeks prior to the hearing—that the car had already been moved. Despite this, the HOA's fine notice, issued after the hearing, stated summarily that "the violation still exists," without providing updated photographs or any substantive rebuttal to your prior communications. This raises concerns as to whether the Committee had current, substantiated evidence to support the imposition of the maximum fine.

• The evidentiary basis for the violations is undermined by the absence of date- and time-stamped photographic evidence: Both the February and July violation notices included images of allegedly offending vehicles. However, neither photograph includes a timestamp, date overlay, or any metadata identifying when the photos were taken. In both instances, the lack of concrete photographic documentation weakens the Association's ability to demonstrate that the violation existed at the relevant time, particularly when you promptly contested the allegations and offered plausible alternative explanations.

• The escalation from an initial to a "final" violation notice raises procedural fairness concerns: The February 10 notice was explicitly labeled as a "First Notice of Deficiency." The July 9 notice, which involved a different vehicle and occurred five months later, was labeled a "Final Notice" with no intermediate communications explaining the escalation or indicating that further violations would trigger an immediate hearing. The Association did not respond to your requests for clarification regarding why this violation was treated as a final step, and no record has been provided showing that progressive enforcement procedures were followed.

• You were not afforded a meaningful opportunity to participate in the hearing or appeal process: Between July 9 and July 17, you sent multiple emails requesting information on how to attend the hearing, how to appeal the violation, and whether the matter was still being pursued given the vehicle had already been removed. No response was received. The fine notice was sent after business hours on July 24—after the hearing had occurred—leaving no opportunity for you to engage with the Committee, present evidence, or request reconsideration before the decision was finalized. The Association's stated commitment to fair, respectful governance, as outlined in its Member Rights and Responsibilities, appears inconsistent with this lack of responsiveness.

• The HOA's strongest argument is that the driveway parking restrictions are explicit, and that compliance—not subjective intent—is controlling: The Association's Rules and Regulations clearly prohibit any obstruction of sidewalks, including the portion that intersects driveways, and expressly forbid "parking vehicles sideways on the driveway approach area." The HOA may argue that the photographic evidence—despite lacking timestamps—visually supports their claim that this rule was violated. Furthermore, the Association may contend that because both violations involved prohibited parking behavior at the same property within a five-month span, the escalation to a hearing and imposition of a fine was procedurally reasonable. They may also assert that their authority to fine up to $100 per day, capped at $1,000, was properly invoked and consistent with the governing documents.

Based on the above, a formal written challenge to the fine—such as a demand letter directed to the Association—would likely be the most efficient and cost-effective next step. This letter would outline the procedural deficiencies surrounding the enforcement process, highlight your repeated and unanswered attempts to seek clarification and comply, and request rescission of the $1,000 fine. It would also seek clarification of the parking rules at issue and request confirmation of your rights with respect to future hearings and communications. While the outcome of such a letter cannot be guaranteed, it may prompt the Association to reconsider the fine or initiate a more constructive dialogue toward resolution.
