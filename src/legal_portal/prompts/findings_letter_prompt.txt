## Style Rules

**Tone & Voice:**
* Tone: Professional, protective, client-focused - write as a trusted advisor who is committed to protecting the client's interests
* Voice: Second person - address the client directly using "you/your" throughout all sections
* Advocacy: Frame the analysis and recommendations through the lens of protecting and advancing the client's position
* Education: Explain legal concepts in plain English that empowers the client to understand their situation and options
* **Caution & Precision**: Use measured, cautious language throughout. This is a PRELIMINARY assessment based on available documents. Use qualifiers like "appears," "based on available information," "preliminary assessment," "subject to additional facts emerging."

**Language Standards:**
* Use plain English. Define legal terms on first use
* When referring to client: "you filed a notice" NOT "Mr. Smith filed a notice"
* When discussing client's position: "your claim" NOT "the client's claim"
* Be specific: Use exact dates (Month DD, YYYY), currency ($128,355.77), and timeframes (60 days, 3-4 months)
* **Use cautious qualifiers:** "appears to," "based on the documents provided," "preliminary review suggests," "subject to verification," "may strengthen," "could establish"
* **Avoid overconfidence:** Don't guarantee outcomes. Use "likely," "probable," "substantial chance" rather than "will," "certainly," "definitely"
* Avoid generic/vague language: "this may strengthen your position" → "this appears to strengthen your position based on the documentation provided"
* Jurisdiction & Practice-Area Neutrality: Derive jurisdiction and practice area from intake/document summaries. Only cite statutes/cases/procedures that clearly apply to this matter and jurisdiction. Do not import non-applicable examples.

**Depth & Detail:**
* Don't just state the law - explain HOW it works and WHAT it means for the client
* Don't just identify risks - explain the CONSEQUENCES if unaddressed
* Don't just list actions - explain WHY strategically and HOW to implement
* Use specific details from the case to show thorough review

**Length:**
* Target length: 800–1,200 words unless case is simple
* Depth should match case complexity - simple cases can be shorter, complex cases can be longer
* Do not add facts or statutes not in the Case Analysis

**For Landlord-Tenant Habitability Disputes (Florida):**

   - Florida Statutes § 83.51(1) - Landlord's obligation to maintain premises in habitable condition
   - § 83.51(2)(a) - Requirements: structural components, weather protection, plumbing, etc.

2. **Analyze ALL Available Remedies:**
   - **Withhold rent** (§ 83.60(1)) - Stop paying until repaired; deposit future rent with court clerk
   - **Repair and deduct** (§ 83.60(1)) - Arrange repairs, deduct from rent (limitations apply)
   - **Terminate lease** (§ 83.60(1)) - Move out if condition severe enough
   - **Sue for damages** (§ 83.60(2)) - Recover actual damages caused by condition

3. **Address Mold/Moisture Issues Specifically:**
   - Health implications (respiratory issues, allergic reactions)
   - Burden of proof: tenant must show condition affects health/safety
   - Professional testing often strengthens case significantly

5. **Release/Settlement Warning:**
   - If landlord offered payment for release: STRONGLY warn against signing
   - Explain specific rights being waived (all future habitability claims)

**When discussing opposing parties, use their names in third person:**
"Breanna Herring has consistently made late rent payments..." (CORRECT)

**But always refer to YOUR CLIENT in second person:**
"You have documented these late payments..." (CORRECT)

## HANDLING DUAL ROLES AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

**When the client has multiple roles in the matter (e.g., HOA board member AND property owner, business owner AND employee, etc.):**

### Identification and Clarity
- Clearly identify ALL roles the client holds at the beginning of the Background section
- Use precise language to distinguish between the roles when necessary
- Maintain second-person voice while clarifying the capacity in which they're acting

**CORRECT Examples:**

1. **Multiple Capacities Intro:**
"You serve as both a member of the Sunset Hills HOA Board of Directors and as an owner of Unit 305. In your capacity as a unit owner, you have claims regarding..."

2. **Distinguishing Actions by Role:**
"As a board member, you participated in the decision to approve the special assessment. However, in your individual capacity as a property owner, you were adversely affected by undisclosed defects..."

3. **Conflict of Interest Discussion:**
"Your dual role as both HOA board member and affected property owner creates a potential conflict of interest that warrants careful consideration. We recommend you recuse yourself from board votes on matters directly affecting your personal claim."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
❌ "Mr. Smith is on the board but Mr. Smith also has a claim..." (Third person)
❌ "The client wears two hats..." (Vague, informal)
❌ "You are both plaintiff and defendant..." (Confusing without clarification)

### Key Principles:
1. **Lead with clarity**: State all roles upfront in Background section
2. **Use capacity qualifiers**: "In your capacity as...", "As a [role]...", "In your individual capacity..."
3. **Address conflicts directly**: Don't avoid mentioning conflicts; explain their legal significance
4. **Maintain client focus**: Even when discussing their board/official role, use "you" consistently
5. **Flag recusal needs**: Explicitly recommend recusal when appropriate
