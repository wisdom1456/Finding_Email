Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. You may have claims under this implied warranty due to [specific defects with citations], failure to meet industry standards, and [other specific issues].

**Pre-Litigation Requirements:**
Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific steps:
• You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair
• The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline
• You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process

This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.

**CONSEQUENCE CHAIN FORMAT (for serious escalating risks):**

For risks that could escalate (liens, foreclosure, eviction, statute of limitations), use this pattern to show progression:

**Structure:** [Triggering Event] → [Intermediate Step] → [Legal Action] → [Ultimate Consequence]

**Example:**
"You have received a Notice to Owner from a subcontractor [Source: Notice.pdf]. This notice is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. In Florida, once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. While this is not always immediate, the risk should not be taken lightly—foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved."

**Why This Works:**
- Names the specific document received
- Shows step-by-step progression with "first step" language
- Uses cautious qualifiers ("not always immediate")
- States ultimate consequence in plain English (FORCED SALE)
- Creates urgency without panic

**When to Use Consequence Chains:**
- Mechanic's liens (Notice to Owner → Lien → Foreclosure → Sale)
- Eviction notices (3-Day Notice → 7-Day Notice → Court Filing → Eviction)
- Statute of limitations (Claim accrued → Time passing → Deadline → Loss of rights forever)
- License suspension (Citation → Hearing → Finding → Suspension)

Target for entire section: 400-600 words across all numbered subsections]

## Style Rules

**Tone & Voice:**
* Professional, protective, client-focused - write as a trusted advisor committed to protecting client interests
* Voice: Second person - address the client using "you/your" throughout all sections
* Use "I" for attorney actions: "I am providing", "I recommend", "I wanted to follow up"
* Use "our firm", "our office" for institutional references
* Use cautious qualifiers: "appears to," "based on available information," "may strengthen," "could establish"

**Language Standards:**
* Plain English first, then technical details
* When referring to client: "you filed a notice" NOT "Mr. Smith filed a notice"
* Be specific: Use exact dates (Month DD, YYYY), currency ($128,355.77), and timeframes
* Avoid overconfidence: Use "likely," "probable," "may" rather than "will," "certainly," "definitely"
* Jurisdiction awareness: Only cite statutes/cases that clearly apply to this matter

**Bullet Point Format:**
Each bullet in Section 2 (Key Legal Points) must be SUBSTANTIVE - a complete paragraph explaining the issue, NOT just a heading.

❌ BAD:
• Breach of Contract
  To establish breach, you must prove...
  
✅ GOOD:
• Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all work will be performed in a competent manner. You may have claims under this warranty due to defective construction, failure to meet industry standards, and the contractor's inability to complete the project despite receiving $100,000 in payments.

**Depth Requirements:**
* Explain HOW the law works, not just WHAT it is
* Explain CONSEQUENCES of risks with real-world impact
* Provide SPECIFIC actions in recommendations
* Use details from case documents to show thorough review
