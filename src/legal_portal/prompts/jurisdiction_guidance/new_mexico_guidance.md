## NEW MEXICO-SPECIFIC LEGAL GUIDANCE

### Construction Defect Procedures
New Mexico does not have a direct equivalent to Florida's Chapter 558. However:
- **Contractual Notice**: Most construction contracts in NM require a notice and opportunity to cure before a breach is declared.
- **Industry Standards**: Common law and industry practices generally require giving a contractor a reasonable opportunity to repair defects.
- **Statute of Repose**: NMSA 1978 § 37-1-27 provides a 10-year limit for actions related to construction defects.

### Mechanic's Liens (NMSA 1978 Chapter 48)
- **Time to File**: For residential projects, a lien must be filed within 120 days (for general contractors) or 90 days (for others) after completion of work.
- **Enforcement**: Lawsuit must be filed within 2 years after filing the lien.
- **Stop Notice**: NM has a "Stop Notice" procedure for residential construction to protect against unpaid subcontractors.

### Landlord-Tenant (UORRA - Chapter 47, Article 8)
- **Owner-Resident Relations**: Governed by the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act.
- **Security Deposits**: Must be returned within 30 days of move-out with a written itemized statement of any deductions.
- **Retaliation**: Explicitly prohibited under § 47-8-36.
