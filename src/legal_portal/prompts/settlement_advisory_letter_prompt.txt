REQUIRED LETTER STRUCTURE:

1. **Header Section**:
   - Date: [Current date in format: Month DD, YYYY]
   - Blank line
   - Client name and address (from client context)
   - Blank line
   - "**RE: Case Assessment and Settlement Recommendation - [Brief Case Description]**"
   - "**PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL - ATTORNEY-CLIENT COMMUNICATION**"
   - Blank line

2. **Salutation**: 
   - `Dear {client_name}:`
   - Blank line

3. **Introduction Paragraph**:
   - Thank the client for their patience during the review process
   - State that after thorough analysis, the firm wishes to share its assessment
   - Note that this letter presents options for the client's consideration
   - Emphasize that the final decision rests with the client

4. **Case Assessment** (2-3 paragraphs):
   - Use heading: "## Case Assessment"
   - Provide honest assessment of case strength
   - Summarize key strengths that support the client's position
   - Acknowledge challenges or weaknesses that may affect outcomes
   - Note any evidentiary gaps that could impact litigation success

5. **Litigation Path Analysis**:
   - Use heading: "## Litigation Considerations"
   - Discuss what full litigation would involve:
     - Estimated timeline (months/years)
     - General cost considerations (without specific numbers)
     - Discovery process and its demands on client
     - Trial risks and uncertainties
   - Note potential outcomes (both positive and negative scenarios)
   - Mention that even strong cases carry inherent risks

6. **Settlement Recommendation**:
   - Use heading: "## Settlement Recommendation"
   - Opening: "Based on our analysis, we recommend exploring settlement negotiations for the following reasons:"
   - Numbered list of 3-5 specific reasons:
     1. [Reason related to case-specific factors]
     2. [Reason related to cost/benefit analysis]
     3. [Reason related to time and certainty]
     4. [Reason related to preserving relationships if applicable]
     5. [Reason related to achieving core client objectives]
   - Note that settlement allows control over the outcome
   - Emphasize that settlement is not "giving up" but strategic decision-making

7. **Settlement Approach**:
   - Use heading: "## Proposed Approach"
   - Outline potential settlement strategy:
     - Initial demand positioning
     - Negotiation timeline
     - Key terms to prioritize
   - Note: "Settlement range and specific terms to be discussed in our consultation"
   - Mention willingness to pursue litigation if settlement fails

8. **Client Options**:
   - Use heading: "## Your Options"
   - Present three clear paths:
     1. **Pursue Settlement**: Authorize firm to initiate negotiations
     2. **Proceed with Litigation**: Move forward with formal legal action
     3. **Hybrid Approach**: Prepare for litigation while exploring settlement
   - Briefly note pros and cons of each
   - Request a consultation to discuss preferences

9. **Next Steps**:
   - Use heading: "## Recommended Next Steps"
   - Request a phone or in-person meeting to discuss options
   - Note any time-sensitive considerations (statutes of limitations, etc.)
   - Provide available times or ask client to schedule

10. **Closing Paragraph**:
    - Blank line
    - Reaffirm commitment to client's best interests
    - Note that the firm will support whichever path client chooses
    - Encourage questions and open discussion
    - Blank line

11. **Signature Block**:
    - "Respectfully,"
    - Blank line
    - "/s/ {attorney_name}"
    - "{attorney_name}, Esq."
    - "{firm_name}"
    - "Attorney for {client_name}"

---

TONE GUIDELINES:
- Professional and thoughtful
- Honest but not discouraging
- Strategic and solution-oriented
- Respectful of client's ultimate decision authority
- Balanced presentation of options

FORMAT NOTES:
- Use markdown formatting (## for headings, - for bullets, numbered lists)
- Keep paragraphs focused and readable
- Present information objectively
- Avoid definitive predictions about case outcomes
- Use hedging language appropriately ("may," "could," "potentially")
