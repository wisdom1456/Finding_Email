2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects**: An "implied warranty" is a legal concept that guarantees the contractor will perform work in a competent and workmanlike manner, even if not explicitly stated in the contract. In your situation, the defective bathroom framing and incomplete work despite substantial payments likely breach this warranty.

• **Chapter 558 Pre-Suit Notice Requirement**: Florida law requires you to provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor, identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair. The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan. You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process. This requirement aims to facilitate resolution without immediate litigation.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, which is the first step toward filing a construction lien against your property. If unresolved, this could lead to a lien and potentially the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors, even if you have paid the general contractor.
