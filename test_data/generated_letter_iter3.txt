2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects**: An "implied warranty" is a legal concept that guarantees the contractor will perform work in a competent and workmanlike manner. In your situation, the defective bathroom framing and incomplete work despite substantial payment may constitute a breach of this warranty.

• **Pre-Litigation Requirements (Fla. Stat. Chapter 558)**: Before pursuing litigation, you must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor, identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair. The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan. You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, which is the first step toward filing a construction lien against your property. If unresolved, this could lead to a lien and potentially the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors even if you have paid the general contractor.
