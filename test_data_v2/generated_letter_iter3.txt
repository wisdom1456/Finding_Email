2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558)**: Under Florida law, an implied warranty exists that all construction work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. This means that the contractor guaranteed to do the work properly, even if the contract doesn't say so directly. You may have claims under this implied warranty due to defective bathroom framing, incomplete work despite the $100,000 payment, and failure to meet industry standards.

**Pre-Litigation Requirements:**
Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific steps:
• You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair.
• The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline.
• You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process.

This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber. This notice is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. The subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. Once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. Foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors even if you already paid the general contractor in full.
