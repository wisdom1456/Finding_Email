2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558):** An "implied warranty" is a legal concept. It is a warranty that is not explicitly stated in your contract but is implied by the law. Essentially, this means that the contractor guaranteed to do the work in a proper and workmanlike way, even if the contract doesn't say so directly. In your situation, the defective bathroom framing and incomplete work despite $100,000 in payments suggest a breach of this implied warranty. Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific steps. You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair. The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline. You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process. This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.

• **Breach of Contract:** The contract with LLW Construction, Inc. established specific obligations, which have not been fulfilled. The failure to complete the project and the substandard quality of work, despite substantial payments, constitutes a breach. Remedies may include seeking the cost to complete the work or reimbursement for hiring another contractor.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner:** You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, which is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. The subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. Once a lien is recorded, they may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. Foreclosure of a construction lien can ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors even if you already paid the general contractor in full, pursuant to Florida Statutes § 713.02 and § 713.06.
