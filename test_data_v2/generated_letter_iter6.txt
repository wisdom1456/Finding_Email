2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Florida Statutes Chapter 558)**: An "implied warranty" is a legal concept that guarantees all construction work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner. In your situation, the defective bathroom framing and incomplete work despite the $100,000 payment suggest a breach of this warranty. Before pursuing litigation, Florida Statutes Chapter 558 requires specific steps:
  - You must provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair.
  - The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline.
  - You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process. This pre-suit requirement protects both parties but means immediate litigation is not possible without first attempting resolution.

• **Breach of Contract**: The contract with LLW Construction, Inc. was for a specified scope of work, which remains incomplete. The failure to complete the project and the substandard quality of work constitute a breach of contract. You may seek remedies such as the cost to complete the work or reimbursement for hiring another contractor.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, which is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. The subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. Once a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to enforce the lien and recover the amount owed. This could ultimately lead to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors even if you have already paid the general contractor in full.

One option would be to issue a demand letter to LLW Construction, Inc. demanding completion of the project in accordance with the contract terms or reimbursement for amounts you may need to pay to hire another contractor. We recommend sending this demand promptly to preserve your legal position. This may lead to a joint resolution that includes mutual waivers and a clear release of future liability.
