2. KEY LEGAL POINTS

Here are the key points of our analysis:

• **Implied Warranty & Construction Defects**: An "implied warranty" is a legal concept that guarantees the work will be performed in a competent and workmanlike manner, even if not explicitly stated in the contract. In your situation, the defective bathroom framing and incomplete work despite the $100,000 payment may constitute a breach of this warranty. You may seek remedies such as the cost to complete the work or repair the defects.

• **Pre-Litigation Requirements (Fla. Stat. Chapter 558)**: Before pursuing litigation, Florida law requires you to provide 60 days' written notice to the contractor, identifying the defects and giving them an opportunity to repair. The contractor then has 45 days to respond with a proposed repair plan and timeline. You CANNOT file suit without completing this statutory notice-and-opportunity-to-repair process. This requirement aims to resolve disputes amicably before resorting to litigation.

• **Mechanic's Liens and Notice to Owner**: You received a Notice to Owner from Tibbetts Lumber, which is the FIRST STEP toward filing a construction lien against your property. The subcontractor has 90 days from their last work date to file a lien. If a lien is recorded, the subcontractor may initiate a foreclosure action to recover the amount owed, potentially leading to the FORCED SALE OF YOUR HOME if not resolved. Florida law may hold you liable for unpaid subcontractors even if you have paid the general contractor in full.
