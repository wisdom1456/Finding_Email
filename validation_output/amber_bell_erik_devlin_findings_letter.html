LEGAL ISSUES

BREACH OF CONTRACT FOR FAILURE TO PERFORM WORK AS AGREED

Under Florida law, a breach of contract occurs when one party fails to fulfill its obligations under the contract. In your case, LLW Construction’s failure to complete the renovations as agreed upon in the contract constitutes a potential breach. The contract specified a total price of $128,335.77, with $100,000 already paid by you. The incomplete work and substandard quality noted since December 2024 support a claim for breach of contract. You may be entitled to monetary damages, reimbursement for additional costs incurred, and other remedies under Florida law.

POSSIBLE CLAIMS FOR DAMAGES DUE TO INCOMPLETE OR SUBSTANDARD WORK

You may also have claims for damages due to the incomplete or substandard work performed by LLW Construction. The issues with work quality and the subcontractor’s non-payment claim further support this potential cause of action. Florida law allows for recovery of costs incurred to correct or complete the work, as well as any additional expenses resulting from the contractor’s failure to perform as agreed.

RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS

A. Issue a Demand Letter

We recommend issuing a formal demand letter to LLW Construction. This letter will notify the contractor of the breach and provide an opportunity to resolve the matter without litigation. It will also serve as a record of your efforts to settle the dispute amicably. The demand letter should outline the incomplete work, the payments made, and your request for reimbursement or completion of the project.

B. Pay the Subcontractor to Avoid a Lien

Given the subcontractor’s claim of non-payment, it is advisable to consider paying the subcontractor directly to avoid a lien on your property. This action would prevent the recording of a lien, which could cloud your property title and create additional legal exposure. If you proceed with payment, ensure you obtain a written release of lien or lien waiver from the subcontractor and retain proof of payment. This payment can be included in your damages claim against LLW Construction.

C. Gather Additional Documentation

To strengthen your position, gather additional documentation and evidence related to the project. This includes any correspondence with LLW Construction, invoices, receipts, and photos of the work performed. These documents will be crucial in supporting your claims and demonstrating the contractor’s failure to fulfill their obligations.
