LEGAL ISSUES

BREACH OF CONTRACT

A breach of contract occurs when one party fails to fulfill their obligations under a contract. In your case, LLW Construction’s failure to complete the renovations and address quality issues constitutes a potential breach. To establish this claim, you must demonstrate that a valid contract exists, a material breach occurred, and you sustained damages as a result. The contract signed on November 14, 2024, and the $100,000 payment support your claim. You may be entitled to monetary damages, reimbursement for additional repair costs, and other remedies under Florida law.

NEGLIGENCE

Negligence involves a failure to exercise reasonable care, resulting in damage or harm. LLW Construction’s substandard work and failure to complete the project may constitute negligence. You must show that LLW Construction owed you a duty of care, breached that duty, and caused damages. The photographic evidence of incomplete and poor-quality work supports this claim. Remedies may include compensation for repair costs and any additional expenses incurred.

UNJUST ENRICHMENT

Unjust enrichment occurs when one party benefits at another’s expense without a legal justification. LLW Construction received $100,000 but failed to deliver the agreed-upon services. This claim requires proving that you conferred a benefit on LLW Construction, they accepted and retained the benefit, and it would be inequitable for them to retain it without payment. You may seek restitution for the payments made for incomplete work.

RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS

A. Issue a Demand Letter

We recommend issuing a demand letter to LLW Construction. This letter will formally notify them of your claims and provide an opportunity to resolve the matter without litigation. It will demand completion of the project, reimbursement for payments made, and compensation for additional costs. This step serves as a record of your attempts to settle the dispute amicably and may facilitate a resolution.

B. Pay the Subcontractor to Avoid a Lien

To protect your property from a potential lien, consider paying the subcontractor directly for the amount owed. This action would prevent the recording of a lien, avoid legal fees, and protect your property title. Ensure you obtain a written release of lien from the subcontractor and retain proof of payment. This payment can be included in your damages claim against LLW Construction.

C. Gather Additional Documentation

Continue gathering documentation to strengthen your position. This includes retaining all communication records, receipts, and any additional evidence of the contractor’s failure to perform. This documentation will support your claims and assist in any future legal proceedings.
