Key Provisions

Breach of Contract:

  • A valid contract exists between you and LLW Construction, Inc. The contractor has materially breached this contract by failing to complete the work and not meeting the required standards [Source: Devlin-ContractforConstruction-HighlightedwItemsnotCompleted6.9.256e0c532b.pdf].
  • Remedies may include monetary damages, reimbursement for payments made, and the cost of hiring a replacement contractor.

Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Fla. Stat. Ch. 558):

  • Florida law implies a warranty that construction work must be performed competently and in a workmanlike manner.
  • Before filing suit, you must serve a statutory 60-day written notice giving the contractor an opportunity to repair. The contractor has 45 days to respond.

Liens and Notice to Owner (Fla. Stat. § 713.02 and § 713.06):

  • Subcontractors who have not been paid may record a lien against your property.
  • A Notice to Owner is a statutory prerequisite to filing such a lien. Even if you have already paid the general contractor, you may still be liable to subcontractors.
  • Failure to resolve a lien can result in foreclosure proceedings.

Analysis

The facts support a breach of contract claim and potential claims for defective construction under Florida’s implied warranty law. The contractor’s failure to perform, combined with the risk of subcontractor liens, creates significant legal and financial exposure if left unaddressed. You have grounds to demand that LLW Construction either cure the defects or reimburse you for the amounts necessary to complete and correct the work. Paying the subcontractor directly—while unfair given you have already paid the contractor—would mitigate lien risk and strengthen your damages claim against LLW Construction. Proceeding under Florida’s Chapter 558 notice process is required before litigation and may provide leverage for settlement without court intervention.

Recommended Next Steps

Letter of Representation & Demand: Our firm can send a formal letter to LLW Construction placing them on notice of defects under Chapter 558, demanding completion of the work, and seeking reimbursement for subcontractor payments.

Payment to Subcontractor: To protect your property from liens and potential foreclosure risk, we recommend paying the subcontractor directly. This should only be done with a written lien release or waiver, and with proof of payment retained.

Preserve Your Rights: Document all deficiencies, communications, and costs incurred. These will support your damages claim.
