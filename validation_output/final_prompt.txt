- Example Letter (style reference): Dear Mr. Devlin and Ms. Bell:

Thank you for taking time out to upload your documents and provide background on your matter. We have completed our review. Below, please find our assessment and recommended next steps.

### Background & Issue

On (date), you engaged LLW Construction, Inc. to perform repairs to your home at 3414 South Belcher Drive, Tampa, FL 33629, following damage from Hurricane Helene. The contract was for $128,355.77, of which $100,000 has already been paid.

Despite this substantial payment, the project remains incomplete and much of the work performed has been defective. For example, the bathroom framing was done improperly, requiring you to reconstruct it yourself. Since January, the contractor has made little progress and has only requested additional money, stating they are out of funds.

You also received a Notice to Owner from a subcontractor claiming over $3,000 in unpaid work, signaling the possibility of a lien being placed on your property.

### Key Provisions
**Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Fla. Stat. Ch. 558):**
* Florida law implies a warranty that work must be performed in a competent, workmanlike manner.
* Before filing suit, you must serve a statutory 60-day written notice giving the contractor an opportunity to repair. The contractor has 45 days to respond.
**Breach of Contract:**
* A valid contract exists, and LLW Construction has materially breached by failing to complete the work and failing to meet required standards.
* Remedies may include monetary damages, reimbursement for subcontractor payments, and the cost of hiring a replacement contractor.
**Liens and Notice to Owner (Fla. Stat. § 713.02 and § 713.06):**
* Subcontractors who have not been paid may record a lien against your property.
* A Notice to Owner is a statutory prerequisite to filing such a lien. Even if you have already paid the general contractor, you may still be liable to subcontractors.
* Failure to resolve a lien can result in foreclosure proceedings.

### Analysis

The facts support both a breach of contract claim and claims for defective construction under Florida’s implied warranty law. The contractor’s failure to perform, combined with the risk of subcontractor liens, creates significant legal and financial exposure if left unaddressed.

You have grounds to demand that LLW Construction either cure the defects or reimburse you for the amounts necessary to complete and correct the work. Paying the subcontractor directly—while unfair given you have already paid the contractor—would mitigate lien risk and strengthen your damages claim against LLW Construction.

Proceeding under Florida’s Chapter 558 notice process is required before litigation and may provide leverage for settlement without court intervention.

### Recommended Next Steps
**Letter of Representation & Demand:** Our firm can send a formal letter to LLW Construction placing them on notice of defects under Chapter 558, demanding completion of the work, and seeking reimbursement for subcontractor payments.
**Payment to Subcontractor:** To protect your property from liens and potential foreclosure risk, we recommend paying the subcontractor directly (~$3,000). This should only be done with a written lien release or waiver, and with proof of payment retained.
**Preserve Your Rights:** Document all deficiencies, communications, and costs incurred. These will support your damages claim.

Please let us know if you would like us to proceed with the Letter of Representation and Demand Letter on your behalf, or if you would prefer to schedule a call to discuss these next steps in more detail.

**Example Letter for Style Reference:**
Dear Mr. Devlin and Ms. Bell:

Thank you for taking time out to upload your documents and provide background on your matter. We have completed our review. Below, please find our assessment and recommended next steps.

### Background & Issue

On (date), you engaged LLW Construction, Inc. to perform repairs to your home at 3414 South Belcher Drive, Tampa, FL 33629, following damage from Hurricane Helene. The contract was for $128,355.77, of which $100,000 has already been paid.

Despite this substantial payment, the project remains incomplete and much of the work performed has been defective. For example, the bathroom framing was done improperly, requiring you to reconstruct it yourself. Since January, the contractor has made little progress and has only requested additional money, stating they are out of funds.

You also received a Notice to Owner from a subcontractor claiming over $3,000 in unpaid work, signaling the possibility of a lien being placed on your property.

### Key Provisions
**Implied Warranty & Construction Defects (Fla. Stat. Ch. 558):**
* Florida law implies a warranty that work must be performed in a competent, workmanlike manner.
* Before filing suit, you must serve a statutory 60-day written notice giving the contractor an opportunity to repair. The contractor has 45 days to respond.
**Breach of Contract:**
* A valid contract exists, and LLW Construction has materially breached by failing to complete the work and failing to meet required standards.
* Remedies may include monetary damages, reimbursement for subcontractor payments, and the cost of hiring a replacement contractor.
**Liens and Notice to Owner (Fla. Stat. § 713.02 and § 713.06):**
* Subcontractors who have not been paid may record a lien against your property.
* A Notice to Owner is a statutory prerequisite to filing such a lien. Even if you have already paid the general contractor, you may still be liable to subcontractors.
* Failure to resolve a lien can result in foreclosure proceedings.

### Analysis

The facts support both a breach of contract claim and claims for defective construction under Florida’s implied warranty law. The contractor’s failure to perform, combined with the risk of subcontractor liens, creates significant legal and financial exposure if left unaddressed.

You have grounds to demand that LLW Construction either cure the defects or reimburse you for the amounts necessary to complete and correct the work. Paying the subcontractor directly—while unfair given you have already paid the contractor—would mitigate lien risk and strengthen your damages claim against LLW Construction.

Proceeding under Florida’s Chapter 558 notice process is required before litigation and may provide leverage for settlement without court intervention.

### Recommended Next Steps
**Letter of Representation & Demand:** Our firm can send a formal letter to LLW Construction placing them on notice of defects under Chapter 558, demanding completion of the work, and seeking reimbursement for subcontractor payments.
**Payment to Subcontractor:** To protect your property from liens and potential foreclosure risk, we recommend paying the subcontractor directly (~$3,000). This should only be done with a written lien release or waiver, and with proof of payment retained.
**Preserve Your Rights:** Document all deficiencies, communications, and costs incurred. These will support your damages claim.

Please let us know if you would like us to proceed with the Letter of Representation and Demand Letter on your behalf, or if you would prefer to schedule a call to discuss these next steps in more detail.
