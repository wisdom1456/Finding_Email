LEGAL ISSUES

MISREPRESENTATION

Under Florida real estate law, sellers are required to disclose known defects or issues that could materially affect the property’s value. Misrepresentation occurs when a seller provides false information or fails to disclose such material facts. In your case, the evidence suggests that William Lichtenstein may have misrepresented the property’s flood history in the disclosure forms. This misrepresentation could entitle you to seek compensation for the damages and loss of usage you have incurred. The photographic evidence and insurance claim documents support your position by demonstrating the extent of the flooding and the associated financial impact.

FAILURE TO DISCLOSE

Failure to disclose material facts, such as a known history of flooding, can also form the basis for a legal claim under Florida law. The seller’s alleged failure to disclose these issues, despite evidence suggesting prior knowledge, strengthens your case. This non-disclosure has resulted in significant financial and emotional distress, as outlined in your correspondence and the insurance claim summary. You may be entitled to recover damages for the financial losses and diminished property value resulting from this failure to disclose.
