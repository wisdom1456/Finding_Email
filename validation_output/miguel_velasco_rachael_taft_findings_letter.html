LEGAL ISSUES MISREPRESENTATION Under Florida law, a seller is required to disclose known facts that materially affect the value of the property and are not readily observable. Misrepresentation occurs when false statements are made that induce another to enter into a contract. - **Standard**: The seller must have made a false statement or omitted a material fact. - **Application**: The seller’s failure to disclose the water damage and structural issues constitutes a potential misrepresentation. - **Defenses**: The seller may argue that the defects were either unknown to them or were observable upon reasonable inspection. - **Remedies**: You may be entitled to rescind the contract, seek damages for repair costs, or recover the diminished value of the property. FAILURE TO DISCLOSE Florida law imposes a duty on sellers to disclose latent defects that are not visible or apparent to the buyer. - **Standard**: Sellers must disclose any known latent defects that materially affect the property’s value. - **Application**: The undisclosed water damage and structural issues are latent defects that should have been disclosed. - **Defenses**: The seller might claim ignorance of these defects or assert that they were disclosed in the documentation. - **Remedies**: Remedies may include rescission of the sale, compensation for repair costs, or damages for the loss in property value. 3.
